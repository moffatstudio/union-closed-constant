## (a) Abstract and main statement, exact

**Abstract.** "We provide a simple proof of a curious inequality for the binary entropy function, an inequality that has been used in two different contexts. In the 1980's, Boppana used this entropy inequality to prove lower bounds on Boolean formulas. More recently, the inequality was used to achieve major progress on Frankl's union-closed sets conjecture. Our proof of the entropy inequality uses basic differential calculus."

**Definition.** $h(x) = -x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x)$ for $0<x<1$; $h(0)=h(1)=0$. Here $\log$ is **natural** logarithm (base $e$) — note this differs from Gilmer/Sawin/AHS/Chase–Lovett's convention of base-2 $\log$, though it does not affect the inequality's validity (only a constant rescaling of $h$).

**Lemma (the main result, stated twice, identically, once in the intro and once restated to open the proof section).** If $0\le x\le1$, then
$$h(x^2)\ \ge\ \phi\,x\,h(x),$$
where $\phi$ is the golden ratio, $\dfrac{\sqrt5+1}{2}$.

(NB on notation: this $\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\approx1.618$ is the golden ratio itself, the *reciprocal* of AHS's $\varphi=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\approx0.618$ and of Sawin's $\phi^{-1}=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$. Boppana's inequality $h(x^2)\ge\phi x h(x)$ at $x=\phi^{-1}=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$ becomes an equality — this $x=\phi^{-1}=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$ is exactly AHS's $\varphi$ and Sawin's $\phi^{-1}$, the extremal point throughout the literature.)

## History section (Introduction), verbatim in substance

- Boppana [refs 2,3 — 1985 FOCS paper and 1989 journal version] first used a **two-variable** inequality on binary entropy to prove Boolean-formula lower bounds; the one-variable inequality above is a special case. That two-variable proof was **computer-assisted**.
- In unpublished 1989 notes [ref 4], Boppana gave a simple (non-computer) proof of the *one-variable* inequality — this is the proof recorded in the present note.
- "More than 30 years later, the same inequality was used to make major progress on Frankl's union-closed sets conjecture." Gilmer's entropy breakthrough was immediately followed by improvements from AHS, Chase–Lovett, Pebody, and Sawin, all of which rely on this entropy inequality.
- **Status of the inequality's proof in each of those papers, as summarized by Boppana himself:**
 - AHS: proof "using computer assistance" (i.e. their Appendix A / Claim 3, verified to $10^{-3}$ via interval-arithmetic-rigorizable numerics).
 - Chase–Lovett: cite AHS's proof (their Claim 2.1).
 - Pebody: literally wrote "to be proven" (Lemmas 5–6 of arXiv:2211.13139, left unproved).
 - Sawin: gave a symbolic proof (his Lemma 8), but himself noted it is "somewhat complicated, though it would not be surprising if a simple symbolic proof exists."
- Boppana's paper "confirms that a simple symbolic proof exists" — this note's entire contribution is exhibiting that simple proof.
- Further progress on the union-closed conjecture cited: Cambie (arXiv:2212.12500), Ellis (arXiv:2211.12401), Yu (arXiv:2212.00658).

## (b) Complete proof transcription (Section 2)

**Extend $h$ to all of $\mathbb{R}$:**
$$h(x) = \begin{cases}-x\log|x|-(1-x)\log|1-x| & x\ne0,1\\ 0 & x=0\text{ or }1\end{cases}.$$

Define $f(x) := h(x^2)-\phi x h(x)$ on $\mathbb{R}$. Goal: show $f\ge0$ on $[0,1]$.

$f'$ is defined on $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{-1,1\}$; $f''$ and $f'''$ on $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}$. Taking three derivatives, for $x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}$:
$$f'''(x) = \frac{p(x)}{x(1-x^2)^2}, \qquad p(x) := -\phi x^3-4x^2+3\phi x+2\phi-4.$$

**Root-counting argument.**
- $p$ has **negative leading coefficient** ($-\phi<0$) and $p(0)=2\phi-4<0$ (since $\phi\approx1.618<2$). A cubic with negative leading coefficient and negative value at $x=0$ must have **at least one negative real root** (as $p(x)\to+\infty$ when $x\to-\infty$ and $p(0)<0$, there's a sign change on $(-\infty,0)$). Hence $p$ has **at most two nonnegative roots**, so $f'''$ has **at most two roots in $(0,1)$**.
- By **Rolle's theorem, applied three times** (going from $f'''$ having $\le2$ roots in $(0,1)$ back up to bounding the roots of $f$ itself): $f$ has **at most five roots in $[0,1]$, counting multiplicity**.
- $f$ is directly verified to have: a **double root at $0$**, a **double root at $\phi^{-1}$**, and a **single root at $1$** — that's five roots (with multiplicity) already found. So these are **all** the roots of $f$ in $[0,1]$; no others exist.
- Since $f$ has a **double** root at $\phi^{-1}$ (an even-order root, not a sign change), $f$ is **either entirely nonnegative or entirely nonpositive on $[0,1]$** (having exhausted all its zeros there, with the interior zero being of even multiplicity so not a genuine sign-crossing).
- For $x$ a tiny positive number, direct check shows $f(x)>0$ (dominant behavior near $0$: $h(x^2)\sim -x^2\log x^2 = -2x^2\log x$ vs. $\phi x h(x)\sim \phi x(-x\log x - x)= -\phi x^2\log x - \phi x^2$; the leading $-2x^2\log x$ term beats $-\phi x^2\log x$ term-by-term coefficient comparison since $2>\phi$... — Boppana's paper states this check without spelling out the asymptotics, simply: "For $x$ a tiny positive number, $f(x)$ is positive.")
- Hence $f\ge0$ throughout $[0,1]$. $\blacksquare$

## (c) Where the inequality is tight / extremal case

**Equality holds exactly at:**
- $x=0$ (double root — both sides $0$);
- $x=\phi^{-1}=\dfrac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\approx0.618$ (double root — the genuine interior tangency point, the golden-ratio point that recurs throughout the entire literature as the extremal probability for the union-closed entropy method);
- $x=1$ (single root — $h(1)=0$ and $\phi\cdot1\cdot h(1)=0$).

This is the same extremal point as: AHS's $\varphi=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$ (where Claim 3's equality $\varphi H(x^2)=xH(x)$ holds at $x\in\{\varphi,1\}$), Chase–Lovett's minimizer $x=\varphi$ of $h(x^2)/(xh(x))$, Sawin's monotonicity-transition point $v=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ (equivalently $s=1-v=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$ in his Lemma 8 substitution), and Pebody's Lemma 6 domain boundary $\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$. Boppana's inequality $h(x^2)\ge\phi x h(x)$ is, after rearrangement ($\phi=1/\varphi$), **exactly equivalent** to the statement "$h(x^2)/(xh(x))\ge1/\varphi$ for all $x\in[0,1]$, with equality iff $x\in\{\varphi,1\}$" (interpreting the $x=0$ double root as a removable-singularity boundary case of the ratio form) — i.e. Boppana's Lemma **is** (a clean restatement/completion of) AHS's Claim 3 / Chase–Lovett's Claim 2.1 / the content underlying Sawin's Lemma 7–8 / Pebody's Lemmas 5–6, now with a fully elementary, non-computer-assisted, non-"to be proven" proof.

## (d) Remarks on limits of the method / open questions

- This is a short technical note whose sole purpose is providing the missing elementary proof of an inequality that **every one of the five union-closed papers above needed** in some form, and which had previously been established only by computer assistance (AHS), by citation to AHS (Chase–Lovett), left as an explicit gap (Pebody: "to be proven"), or by a self-described "somewhat complicated" symbolic argument (Sawin, whose Lemma 8 proof — via a third-derivative/Rolle's-theorem cubic-root-counting argument extremely similar in structure to Boppana's, but requiring an additional global-min/global-max uniqueness argument first) — Boppana's proof is, by his own framing, the clean generalizable technique underlying that whole chain.
- No open questions specific to the union-closed conjecture are raised in this note; it is purely a technical lemma paper. It does note the two historical contexts (1980s Boolean formula lower bounds vs. 2022 union-closed sets) as independent motivations for the same inequality, which is a notable piece of mathematical history: Boppana's own 30-plus-year-old unpublished technique turned out to be exactly what the union-closed literature needed, but had to be independently rediscovered by AHS/Sawin (via computer assistance / a more complicated symbolic route) before Boppana learned of the application and supplied the original elementary proof.
- The proof method itself (Rolle's theorem applied iteratively to bound the total number of sign changes of a difference-of-entropy-type function, via a rational function with polynomial numerator whose leading-coefficient sign and value at a boundary point immediately bound its number of roots) is presented as a reusable technique — the same technique (with more bookkeeping, since it required first separately establishing that $\phi^{-1}$ is a global extremum) is essentially what Sawin's Lemma 8 already used, but Boppana's version needs no separate global-extremum argument since it directly counts all five roots of $f$ by exhibiting them, rather than arguing by contradiction from an assumed extra critical point.
