## (a) Abstract and main theorem statements, exact

**Abstract.** "We show that for any union-closed family $\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]},\mathcal{F}\neq\{\emptyset\}$, there exists an $i\in[n]$ which is contained in a $0.01$ fraction of the sets in $\mathcal{F}$. This is the first known constant lower bound, and improves upon the $\Omega(\log_{2}(|\mathcal{F}|)^{-1})$ bounds of Knill and Wójick. Our result follows from an information theoretic strengthening of the conjecture. Specifically, we show that if $A,B$ are independent samples from a distribution over subsets of $[n]$ such that $Pr[i\in A]<0.01$ for all $i$ and $H(A)>0$, then $H(A\cup B)>H(A)$."

**Theorem 1.** Let $A$ and $B$ denote independent samples from a distribution over subsets of $[n]$. Assume that for all $i\in[n]$, $Pr[i\in A]\leq 0.01$. Then
$$H(A\cup B)\geq 1.26\,H(A).$$

**Theorem 2** (corollary of Theorem 1). Let $\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}$ be a union-closed family, $\mathcal{F}\neq\{\emptyset\}$. Then there exists $i\in[n]$ that is contained in at least a $0.01$ fraction of the sets in $\mathcal{F}$.

**Lemma 1** (the key technical lemma). Let $C$ denote a random variable over a finite set $S$. For each $c\in S$, let $p_c$ be a real number in $[0,1]$. Let $X$ be a Bernoulli random variable sampled by: first sample $c\sim C$, then sample $X$ with $Pr[X=1|C=c]=p_c$. Assume $\mathbb{E}[X]\leq 0.01$. Let $C'$ be an iid copy of $C$, and sample $X'$ conditioned on $C'$ by the same process (so $X'$ independent of $X,C$). Then
$$H(X\cup X'|C,C')\geq 1.26\,H(X|C).$$

Equivalently, for a finite sequence $\{p_c\}_{c\in S}\subset[0,1]$ with $\mathbb{E}_c[p_c]\leq 0.01$:
$$\mathbb{E}_{c,c'}\left[H(p_c+p_{c'}-p_c p_{c'})\right]\geq 1.26\,\mathbb{E}_c[H(p_c)].$$

**Conjecture 1** (Section 5). Let $A,B$ be iid samples from a distribution over a family of subsets of $[n]$. Assume $Pr[i\in A]<0.5$ for all $i$, and $H(A)>0$. Then
$$H(A\cup B)+D(A\cup B\,\|\,A)>H(A).$$
(Note: this conjecture was later **disproved** by Sawin and independently by Ellis — see the Update note below and the Sawin transcription.)

Throughout, $\log$ is base 2. For Bernoulli r.v.s $X,X'$, $X\cup X' := \max(X,X')$. $H(p) := -p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)$ is Shannon/binary entropy.

## (b) Complete proof transcription

### Preliminaries (Section 2)

Two facts used throughout:
1. **Chain rule for entropy**: for $X_1,\dots,X_n$, writing $X_{<i}=(X_1,\dots,X_{i-1})$, $H(X_1,\dots,X_n)=\sum_i H(X_i|X_{<i})$.
2. **Conditioning on less information does not decrease entropy**: for a function $f(Y)$,
$$H(X|Y)\leq H(X|f(Y)).$$
*Proof of (2):* $X\to Y\to f(Y)$ is a Markov chain, so by data processing $I(X:f(Y))\leq I(X:Y)$, i.e. $H(X)-H(X|f(Y))\leq H(X)-H(X|Y)$, i.e. $H(X|Y)\leq H(X|f(Y))$. $\blacksquare$

### Reduction of Theorem 1 to Lemma 1 (Section 3)

Write $A_{<i}=(A_1,\dots,A_{i-1})$ for the indicator sequence ($A_i=1 \iff i\in A$). Strategy: reveal bits of $A\cup B$ and $A$ one at a time, showing at every step
$$H\big((A\cup B)_i\,|\,(A\cup B)_{<i}\big)\;\geq\;1.26\,H(A_i|A_{<i}). \tag{1}$$
Summing via the chain rule gives $H(A\cup B)\ge 1.26\,H(A)$.

**Proof of Theorem 1 from Lemma 1.** First,
$$H\big((A\cup B)_i\,|\,(A\cup B)_{<i}\big)\;\geq\;H\big((A\cup B)_i\,|\,A_{<i},B_{<i}\big) \tag{2}$$
by property (2) above, since $(A\cup B)_{<i}$ is a function of $A_{<i},B_{<i}$.

*Remark, verbatim in spirit of the paper's own emphasis:* "(2) is the crucial step which takes advantage of the power of the information theoretic formulation. Because $(A\cup B)_{<i}$ is simply a function of $A_{<i},B_{<i}$, the entropy in $(A\cup B)_i$ can not increase if we additionally assume we know the full history of $A_{<i},B_{<i}$. Conditioning on $A_{<i},B_{<i}$ dramatically simplifies the analysis, as these are iid." Additionally $A_i,B_i$ are Bernoulli with distribution determined by the realized $A_{<i},B_{<i}$. So Lemma 1 (with $C=A_{<i}$, $C'=B_{<i}$, $X=A_i$, $X'=B_i$, $p_c = \Pr[A_i=1\mid A_{<i}=c]$) gives
$$H\big((A\cup B)_i\,|\,A_{<i},B_{<i}\big)\;\geq\;1.26\,H(A_i|A_{<i}). \tag{3}$$
Chain rule over $i=1,\dots,n$ then gives $H(A\cup B)\geq 1.26\,H(A)$. $\blacksquare$

**Proof of Theorem 2 from Theorem 1 (implicit in intro, made explicit).** Sample $A,B$ uniformly and independently from union-closed $\mathcal{F}\ne\{\emptyset\}$. $A\cup B$ is again a distribution over $\mathcal{F}$ (by union-closure), and the uniform distribution maximizes entropy over any fixed support $\mathcal{F}$, so $H(A\cup B)\le H(A)=\log|\mathcal{F}|$. If every element had $\Pr[i\in A]\le 0.01$, Theorem 1 would give $H(A\cup B)\ge 1.26H(A)>H(A)$ (since $H(A)>0$ as $\mathcal{F}\ne\{\emptyset\}$), a contradiction. Hence some $i$ has $\Pr[i\in A]>0.01$, i.e. is contained in more than a $0.01$ fraction of sets of $\mathcal{F}$.

### Proof of Lemma 1 (Section 4)

Restated: show
$$\mathbb{E}_{c,c'}\big[H(p_c+p_{c'}-p_cp_{c'})\big]\;\geq\;1.26\,\mathbb{E}_c[H(p_c)] \tag{5}$$
under $\mathbb{E}_c[p_c]\le 0.01=\mu$.

**Two failed natural approaches, explicitly discussed** (important context for why the method is "wasteful"):
- *Jensen's inequality attempt:* apply Jensen to $f(p_c,p_{c'})=H(p_c+p_{c'}-p_cp_{c'})-H(p_c)$. Fails: $f$ is not convex in $p_{c'}$. Also false in general that $\mathbb{E}_{c,c'}[H(p_c+p_{c'}-p_cp_{c'})-H(p_c)]\ge H(2\mu-\mu^2)-H(\mu)$ — e.g. if conditioned on $C$ there is no entropy left in $X$, LHS is exactly 0 (this is what happens in the paper's Example 2: after revealing $A_1$, all subsequent bits are deterministic). The natural symmetrization $g(p_c,p_{c'})=H(p_c+p_{c'}-p_cp_{c'})-\tfrac{H(p_c)+H(p_{c'})}{2}$ is likewise not convex.
- *Purely information-theoretic attempt:* hypothesis that for **any** function $f$, $H(f(X,X')|C,C')\ge H(X|C)$ whenever $H(f(X,X'))\ge H(X)$. **False**, via an explicit counterexample: let $X,C$ both be uniform on $\{0,1,2,3\}$; let $X|C$ be uniform on $\{0,2\}$ when $C\in\{0,2\}$ and uniform on $\{1,3\}$ when $C\in\{1,3\}$; let $f(x,x')=(x\bmod 2,\,x'\bmod 2)$. Then $H(f(X,X'))=H(X)=\log 4$, $H(X|C)=1$, but $H(f(X,X')|C,C')=0$. So any valid proof must use specific properties of the union function.

**Direct estimation approach actually used.** Split $S$ into
$$\mathcal{C}_0=\{c : p_c\le 0.1\}, \qquad \mathcal{C}_1=\mathcal{C}_0^c.$$
By Markov's inequality, using $\mathbb{E}[X]\le 0.01$:
$$\Pr[c\in\mathcal{C}_1]=\Pr[p_c>0.1]\le \frac{\mathbb{E}_c[p_c]}{0.1}\le 0.1, \tag{6}$$
so $\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]\ge 0.9$. Decompose (writing $\mathcal{C}_0$ also for the event $C\in\mathcal{C}_0$, etc.):
$$H(X|C)=\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]H(X|\mathcal{C}_0)+\Pr[\mathcal{C}_1]H(X|\mathcal{C}_1).$$

*Proof sketch given first, then made rigorous:* Conditioned on both $C,C'\in\mathcal{C}_0$, $H(X\cup X')$ is a constant factor larger than $\tfrac{H(X)+H(X')}{2}$, giving
$$\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]^2\,H(X\cup X'|\mathcal{C}_0,\mathcal{C}_0')\;\geq\;1.26\,\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]\,H(X|\mathcal{C}_0). \tag{7}$$
Conditioned on exactly one of $c,c'\in\mathcal{C}_0$, one shows $H(X\cup X')\ge 0.9H(X)$, giving
$$2\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]\Pr[\mathcal{C}_1]\,H(X\cup X'|\mathcal{C}_0,\mathcal{C}_1')\;\geq\;1.62\,\Pr[\mathcal{C}_1]\,H(X|\mathcal{C}_1). \tag{8}$$
*Motivation from Example 3 (see below):* most entropy in $H(X|C)$ there comes from the event $A_1=1$ (the $\mathcal{C}_1$ event), and this entropy is dominated by the sub-event that exactly one of $A_1,B_1$ equals 1 — exactly the content of (8). The term $\Pr[\mathcal{C}_1]^2H(X,X'|C,C'\in\mathcal{C}_1)$ (both in $\mathcal{C}_1$) is **thrown away entirely** as merely non-negative — the paper explicitly flags this as wasteful: "In this work we throw this term away, it is non-negative and the sum of the left hand side of (7) and (8) are already larger than $H(X|C)$. However, a tight version of Lemma 1 will require a more careful analysis."

**Lemma 2.** Assume $p,p'\le 0.1$. Then $H(p+p'-pp')\ge 1.4\left(\frac{H(p)+H(p')}{2}\right)$.

*Proof.* Holds trivially at $p=p'=0$. By concavity of $H$, $\frac{H(p)+H(p')}{2}\le H\left(\frac{p+p'}{2}\right)$. For $0\le p,p'\le 0.1$: $p+p'-pp'\ge 0.9(p+p')$. So with $f(p,p')=\frac{2H(p+p'-pp')}{H(p)+H(p')}$, on the domain $f(p,p')\ge \frac{H(0.9(p+p'))}{H(0.5(p+p'))}$. The function $g(t)=\frac{H(0.9t)}{H(0.5t)}$ for $t\in(0,0.2]$ is **minimized at $t=0.2$**, giving $f(p,p')> g(0.2)=1.45$. (A figure in the paper plots $f$ over $[0,0.1]^2$ and notes the minimum value $1.496$ is achieved at $p=p'=0.1$.) $\blacksquare$

**Lemma 3.** For any $p,p'\in[0,1]$, $H(p+p'-pp')\ge (1-p)H(p')$.

*Proof.* By concavity of $H$: $H(p\cdot 1+(1-p)p')\ge pH(1)+(1-p)H(p')=(1-p)H(p')$. Since $p+p'-pp'=p\cdot1+(1-p)p'$, done. $\blacksquare$

Notation for next lemmas: $q(c)=\Pr[C=c]$; $q_0$ = distribution of $C$ conditioned on $C\in\mathcal{C}_0$, i.e. $q_0(c)=q(c)/\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]$ for $c\in\mathcal{C}_0$.

**Lemma 4.** Under $\mathbb{E}[X]\le 0.01$: $\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]^2H(X\cup X'|\mathcal{C}_0,\mathcal{C}_0')\ge 1.26\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]H(X|C\in\mathcal{C}_0)$.

*Proof.*
$$\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]H(X|C\in\mathcal{C}_0)=\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]\,\mathbb{E}_{c\sim q_0}H(p_c) =\frac{\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]}{2}\mathbb{E}_{c\sim q_0}\Big[H(p_c)+\mathbb{E}_{c'\sim q_0}H(p_{c'})\Big]$$
$$=\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]\,\mathbb{E}_{c,c'\sim q_0}\left[\frac{H(p_c)+H(p_{c'})}{2}\right] \overset{\text{Lemma 2}}{\le} \frac{\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]}{1.4}\,\mathbb{E}_{c,c'\sim q_0}H(p_c+p_{c'}-p_cp_{c'}) \overset{\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]\ge0.9}{\le} \frac{\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]^2}{1.26}H(X\cup X'|C,C'\in\mathcal{C}_0).$$
Multiply by $1.26$. $\blacksquare$

**Lemma 5.** Under $\mathbb{E}[X]\le 0.01$: $2\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0,\mathcal{C}_1']H(X\cup X'|\mathcal{C}_0,\mathcal{C}_1')\ge 1.62\Pr[\mathcal{C}_1]H(X|C\in\mathcal{C}_1)$.

*Proof.*
$$2\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0,\mathcal{C}_1']H(X\cup X'|\mathcal{C}_0,\mathcal{C}_1')=2\!\!\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_0,c'\in\mathcal{C}_1}\!\!q(c)q(c')H(p_c+p_{c'}-p_cp_{c'})$$
$$\overset{\text{Lemma 3}}{\ge} 2\!\!\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_0,c'\in\mathcal{C}_1}\!\!q(c)q(c')(1-p_c)H(p_{c'}) = 2\Pr[\mathcal{C}_1']H(X'|\mathcal{C}_1')\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_0}q(c)(1-p_c)$$
$$\overset{p_c\le0.1}{\ge} 2\Pr[\mathcal{C}_1']H(X|\mathcal{C}_1')\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_0}q(c)\cdot0.9 = 1.8\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]\Pr[\mathcal{C}_1']H(X'|\mathcal{C}_1') \overset{\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]\ge0.9}{\ge} 1.62\Pr[\mathcal{C}_1']H(X'|\mathcal{C}_1'). \blacksquare$$

**Completion of Lemma 1's proof.** Write $H(X\cup X'|C,C')$ as a sum over three disjoint events:
1. $\Pr[C,C'\in\mathcal{C}_0]\,H(X\cup X'|C,C'\in\mathcal{C}_0)$ — bounded below by $1.26\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]H(X|\mathcal{C}_0)$ via Lemma 4.
2. $2\Pr[C\in\mathcal{C}_0]\Pr[C'\in\mathcal{C}_1]\,H(X\cup X'|C\in\mathcal{C}_0,C'\in\mathcal{C}_1)$ — bounded below by $1.62\Pr[\mathcal{C}_1]H(X|\mathcal{C}_1)$ via Lemma 5.
3. $\Pr[C,C'\in\mathcal{C}_1]\,H(X\cup X'|C,C'\in\mathcal{C}_1)$ — non-negative, **discarded**.

Sum: $H(X\cup X'|C,C')\ge 1.26\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]H(X|\mathcal{C}_0)+1.62\Pr[\mathcal{C}_1]H(X|\mathcal{C}_1)\ge 1.26\big(\Pr[\mathcal{C}_0]H(X|\mathcal{C}_0)+\Pr[\mathcal{C}_1]H(X|\mathcal{C}_1)\big)=1.26\,H(X|C)$. $\blacksquare$

*Note:* the constant $0.01$ is **not optimized**: "new ideas will be needed to achieve a tight result." The paper hypothesizes: if $\mathbb{E}[X]<\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ then $H(X\cup X'|C,C')\ge (1+\epsilon)H(X|C)$ for some $\epsilon>0$ depending on $\mathbb{E}[X]$ — this is exactly what Sawin/AHS/Pebody/Chase–Lovett later prove.

## (c) Extremal / tightness discussion, illustrative examples (Section 1)

**Example 1.** $A=(A_1,\dots,A_n)$, iid Bernoulli$(p)$ coordinates. Then $H(A)=nH(p)$, $H(A\cup B)=nH(2p-p^2)$.

**Example 2.** $A=[n]$ w.p. $p$, $A=\emptyset$ w.p. $1-p$. Then $H(A)=H(p)$, $H(A\cup B)=H(2p-p^2)$.

In both, the ratio is $\frac{H(A\cup B)}{H(A)}=\frac{H(2p-p^2)}{H(p)}$, and:
- $p<\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$: $H(A\cup B)>H(A)$;
- $p=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$: **equality** $H(A\cup B)=H(A)$;
- $p>\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$: $H(A\cup B)<H(A)$.

This makes Examples 1–2 the **extremal family** pinning down $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ as the exact threshold for the entropy method (this is where the constant comes from across the entire literature). The paper explicitly hypothesizes (later proven) the general inequality $H(A\cup B)\ge \frac{H(2p-p^2)}{H(p)}H(A)$ whenever $\Pr[A_i=1]\le p$ for all $i$.

**Example 3** (motivates the $\mathcal{C}_0/\mathcal{C}_1$ split used in Section 4). Sample $A_1\sim$Bernoulli$(p)$; conditioned on $A_1=1$, sample each other $A_i$ iid Bernoulli$(q{=}0.99)$; if $A_1=0$, all $A_i=0$. Then by chain rule $H(A)=H(A_1)+H(A_{>1}|A_1)$, and $H(A_{>1}|A_1)=\Pr[A_1=0]\cdot0+\Pr[A_1=1]H(q)(n-1)$, so
$$H(A)=H(p)+pH(q)(n-1),$$
$$H(A\cup B)=H(2p-p^2)+2p(1-p)H(q)(n-1)+p^2H(2q-q^2)(n-1).$$
For $n$ large, $p$ small, $H(A\cup B)$ is dominated by $2p(1-p)H(q)(n-1)$ — the event that exactly one of $A_1,B_1$ equals 1 — giving $\frac{H(A\cup B)}{H(A)}\approx 2(1-p)$. Here $H(A\cup B|A_1=B_1=1)$ is small relative to $H(A|A_1=1)$ — i.e. the discarded $\mathcal{C}_1\times\mathcal{C}_1$ term in the Section 4 proof really can be small, justifying (heuristically) throwing it away while showing the method is inherently wasteful there.

Because $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\approx0.382<0.5$, **no strengthening of Theorem 1's hypothesis-conclusion form alone can resolve Frankl's conjecture** — this is stated explicitly: "any stronger bound for Theorem 1 will not be sufficient to resolve the union-closed conjecture."

## (d) Remarks on limits of the method / open questions (Section 5, Section 6, and post-publication Update)

**Section 5 — "A possible path towards resolving the conjecture."** A tight Lemma 1 gives only the $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ bound, which is $<\tfrac12$, so genuinely new ideas are needed. Idea proposed: near $p=\tfrac12$, $A\cup B$ may be far from uniform on $\mathcal{F}$ even when $H(A\cup B)\le H(A)$ (i.e. $|\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}|$, meaning $\{A\cup B\mid A,B\in\mathcal{F}\}$, may still exceed $|\mathcal{F}|$). Quantify via KL divergence $D(A\cup B\|A)$. Identity used: when $A$ is uniform on union-closed $\mathcal{F}$,
$$D(A\cup B\,\|\,A)+H(A\cup B)=H(A)=\log|\mathcal{F}|. \tag{9}$$
(cited to Cover & Thomas, Thm 2.6.4.) For a single bit with $\Pr=p$: at $p=0.5$, $D(A\cup B\|A)+H(A\cup B)=H(A)=1.0$ exactly; for $p<0.5$,
$$D(A\cup B\|A)+H(A\cup B)>H(A). \tag{10}$$
If (10) ever holds for a distribution $A$, then either $A$ is not uniform on $\mathcal{F}$, or $A\cup B$ has support outside $\mathcal{F}$ — either way a contradiction is forced for a hypothetical counterexample family, **if** the following conjecture holds:

**Conjecture 1** (restated): $A,B$ iid over subsets of $[n]$, $\Pr[i\in A]<0.5$ for all $i$, $H(A)>0$ $\Rightarrow$ $H(A\cup B)+D(A\cup B\|A)>H(A)$. This conjecture, if true, **would resolve Frankl's conjecture in full** (constant $1/2$).

**Section 6 — Conclusion.** States the method actually gives $H(A\cup B)\ge C_pH(A)$ for a constant $C_p>0$ depending on $p=\max_i\Pr[A_i=1]$, but "we certainly have not derived the strongest possible bound $C_p$." Lists three open questions:
1. Does $H(A\cup B)\ge \frac{H(2p-p^2)}{H(p)}H(A)$ hold whenever $\Pr[A_i=1]\le p$ for all $i$? (Answered: **yes** for $p\le\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ by Sawin; for $p>\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ only $H(A\cup B)\ge(1-p)\frac{2}{\sqrt5-1}H(A)$ holds, per Sawin.)
2. Does Conjecture 1 hold? (Answered: **no** — disproved by both Sawin and Ellis, arXiv:2211.12401.)
3. Under other assumptions (e.g. $\Pr[X\subseteq A]<p$ for all $k$-subsets $X$), how small must $p$ be for $H(A\cup B)>H(A)$?

**Update (27 Nov 2022), added to the paper itself:** "Shortly after publication of this preprint, three publications appeared which all prove tight versions of our Lemma 1 [Chase–Lovett, Sawin, AHS]. These results improve the resulting bound on Frankl's conjecture to $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\approx.38$." Sawin confirms Question 1 for $p\le\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ but shows for $p>\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ only the weaker $(1-p)\frac{2}{\sqrt5-1}$ bound holds. Sawin and Ellis both give counterexamples refuting Conjecture 1. Gilmer notes: "It is noteworthy that Sawin's construction demonstrates that, without placing additional assumptions on the distribution $A$, incorporating the KL term cannot improve the resulting bound on Frankl's conjecture" — i.e., the entropy-method barrier at $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ is not an artifact of discarding KL information; the proposed Path in Section 5 does **not** work as stated.
