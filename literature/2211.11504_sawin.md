## (a) Abstract and main theorem statements, exact

**Abstract.** "Gilmer has recently shown that in any nonempty union-closed family $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of a finite set, there exists an element contained in at least a proportion $.01$ of the sets of $\mathcal{F}$. We improve the proportion from $.01$ to $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\approx.382$ in this result. An improvement to $\frac12$ would be the Frankl union-closed set conjecture. We follow Gilmer's method, replacing one key estimate by a sharp estimate. We then suggest a new addition to this method and sketch a proof that it can obtain a constant strictly greater than $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$. We also disprove a conjecture of Gilmer that would have implied the union-closed set conjecture."

**Theorem 1.** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a nonempty union-closed family of subsets of $[n]$. Then there exists $i\in[n]$ contained in a proportion at least $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ of the sets in $\mathcal{F}$.

**Theorem 2.** Let $u\in[0,1]$. Let $A$ and $B$ denote independent samples from a distribution over subsets of $[n]$. Assume that, for all $i\in[n]$, $\Pr[i\in A]\le u$. Then
$$H(A\cup B)\ \ge\ H(A)\cdot\begin{cases}\dfrac{H(2u-u^2)}{H(u)} & \text{if } u\le\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\\[4pt] (1-u)\dfrac{2}{\sqrt5-1} & \text{if } u\ge\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\end{cases}.$$

**Lemma 3.** Let $u\in[0,1]$. Let $p,q$ be i.i.d. $[0,1]$-valued random variables with expectation $\le u$. Then
$$\mathbb{E}[H(p+q-pq)]\ \ge\ \mathbb{E}[H(p)]\cdot\begin{cases}\dfrac{H(2u-u^2)}{H(u)} & \text{if } u\le\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\\[4pt] (1-u)\dfrac{2}{\sqrt5-1} & \text{if } u\ge\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\end{cases}.$$

All three (Theorems 1–2, Lemma 3) are exact strengthenings of Gilmer's Theorem 1, Theorem 2, Lemma 1, which had constant $0.01$. Write $\lambda$ for the piecewise expression above ($\lambda=\lambda(u)$).

**Proposition 6 (disproof of Gilmer's Conjecture 1).** For any $u<1$ and $d>\frac{H(2u-u^2)}{H(u)}$, for all sufficiently large $n$, there exists a random variable $A$ valued in subsets of $[n]$, containing each element with probability $\le u$, such that $H(A\cup B)\le dH(A)$, but $D(A\cup B\|A)=O(1)$ while $H(A)$ grows linearly in $n$.

**Question 9.** For any probability measure $\mu$ on subsets of $[n]$, with nonzero entropy, such that $\mu(\{A\subseteq[n]\mid i\in A\})<1/2$ for all $i\in[n]$, do there exist random variables $A,B$, identically distributed with measure $\mu$ but **not necessarily independent**, such that $H(A\cup B)>H(A)$? *A positive answer would imply the union-closed conjecture.*

**Priority/history note (verbatim substance from the paper's own remarks):** "The same day this article first appeared on arXiv, two independent proofs [Chase–Lovett; AHS] of Theorem 1 also did. Since [Chase–Lovett] depended on [AHS] for a key lemma, and the proof of a similar lemma in the first version of this article contained an error, only [AHS] gave a completely independent proof. The next day, an independent construction [Ellis] of a counterexample similar to Proposition 6 (for $n=2$ instead of for fixed $n$) appeared." Also: Sawin thanks Boppana "for pointing me to the techniques for a corrected proof based on his proof in [Boppana 2301.09664... though note: chronologically Boppana's note formalizing this predates its arXiv posting] of a special case sufficient for Theorem 1" — i.e. Lemma 8 below was fixed using Boppana's technique after Bhavik Mehta found an error in the original proof.

## (b) Complete proof transcription

### Section 1 — Proofs

**Proof of Theorem 1 from Theorem 2.** "Identical to [Gilmer, p.2]." Let $A,B$ independent uniform on $\mathcal{F}$. Suppose for contradiction no $i$ has proportion $\ge\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$; then some $u<\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ has $\Pr[i\in A]\le u\ \forall i$. Then
$$H(A\cup B)\ge H(A)\cdot\frac{H(2u-u^2)}{H(u)}>H(A),$$
contradicting maximality of uniform-distribution entropy on $\mathcal{F}$. $\blacksquare$

**Proof of Theorem 2 from Lemma 3.** "Identical to [Gilmer, Proof of Theorem 1 on p.4]." Let $A_{<i}=A\cap[i-1]$, similarly $B_{<i}$, $(A\cup B)_{<i}=A_{<i}\cup B_{<i}$. Prove by induction on $i$:
$$H((A\cup B)_{<i})\ge H(A_{<i})\cdot\lambda \tag{1}$$
Base case $i=1$: both sides $0$. Inductive step: by chain rule,
$$H((A\cup B)_{<i+1})=H((A\cup B)_{<i+1}\mid(A\cup B)_{<i})+H((A\cup B)_{<i}),$$
$$H(A_{<i+1})=H(A_{<i+1}\mid A_{<i})+H(A_{<i}),$$
so, assuming (1) at $i$, it suffices for (1) at $i+1$ to show
$$H((A\cup B)_{<i+1}\mid(A\cup B)_{<i})\ \ge\ \lambda\, H(A_{<i+1}\mid A_{<i}). \tag{2}$$
By data processing, $H((A\cup B)_{<i+1}\mid(A\cup B)_{<i})\ge H((A\cup B)_{<i+1}\mid A_{<i},B_{<i})$. Let $p_i=\Pr[i\in A\mid A_{<i}]$, $q_i=\Pr[i\in B\mid B_{<i}]$; since $A,B$ iid, $p_i,q_i$ are independent, identically distributed, expectation $\le u$. The probability $i\in(A\cup B)_{<i+1}$ given $A_{<i},B_{<i}$ is $p_i+q_i-p_iq_i$. Since $i\in A$ is the only new information from $A_{<i}$ to $A_{<i+1}$: $H(A_{<i+1}\mid A_{<i})=\mathbb{E}[H(p_i)]$ and $H((A\cup B)_{<i+1}\mid A_{<i},B_{<i})=\mathbb{E}[H(p_i+q_i-p_iq_i)]$. (2) then follows from Lemma 3. $\blacksquare$

**Proof of Lemma 3.** Equivalent to: minimum of
$$\mathbb{E}_{(p,q)\sim\mu\times\mu}[H(p+q-pq)]-\lambda\,\mathbb{E}_{p\sim\mu}[H(p)] \tag{3}$$
over probability measures $\mu$ on $[0,1]$ with expectation $\le u$ is nonnegative. (Minimum attained: weak compactness + continuity of the relevant functionals in $\mu$.)

*Step 1: the minimizer $\mu$ also minimizes the "linearized" functional in a second, independent variable $\nu$.* Claim: if $\mu$ minimizes (3), $\mu$ also minimizes
$$2\,\mathbb{E}_{(p,q)\sim\mu\times\nu}[H(p+q-pq)]-\lambda\,\mathbb{E}_{q\sim\nu}[H(q)]=\mathbb{E}_{q\sim\nu}\Big[2\,\mathbb{E}_{p\sim\mu}[H(p+q-pq)]-\lambda H(q)\Big] \tag{4}$$
over $\nu$ with expectation $\le u$. Otherwise, take $\mu'=(1-\epsilon)\mu+\epsilon\nu$; expanding (3) at $\mu'$ to first order in $\epsilon$:
$$\mathbb{E}_{\mu'\times\mu'}[H(p+q-pq)]-\lambda\mathbb{E}_{\mu'}[H(p)] = \big[(3)\text{ at }\mu\big] + \epsilon\Big(2\mathbb{E}_{\mu\times\nu}[H(p+q-pq)]-\lambda\mathbb{E}_\nu[H(p)]-2\mathbb{E}_{\mu\times\mu}[H(p+q-pq)]+\lambda\mathbb{E}_\mu[H(p)]\Big)+O(\epsilon^2).$$
If the coefficient of $\epsilon$ (i.e. (4) at $\nu$ minus (4) at $\mu$) were negative, (3) at $\mu'$ would be $<$ (3) at $\mu$ for small $\epsilon$, contradicting minimality. So $\nu=\mu$ minimizes (4).

*Step 2: analyze $F_\mu(q):=2\,\mathbb{E}_{p\sim\mu}[H(p+q-pq)]-\lambda H(q)$ via its "normalized" second derivative.* $H(q)=-q\log q-(1-q)\log(1-q)$, $H'(q)=-\log q+\log(1-q)$, $H''(q)=-\frac1q-\frac1{1-q}=-\frac1{q(1-q)}$. By chain rule for the linear substitution $q\mapsto p+q-pq$: $\frac{d^2}{dq^2}H(p+q-pq)=-\frac{(1-p)^2}{(p+q-pq)(1-p-q+pq)}=-\frac{1-p}{(p+q-pq)(1-q)}$. So
$$\frac{d^2}{dq^2}F_\mu(q)=-2\,\mathbb{E}_{p\sim\mu}\left[\frac{1-p}{(p+q-pq)(1-q)}\right]+\frac{\lambda}{q(1-q)},$$
$$q(1-q)\frac{d^2}{dq^2}F_\mu(q)=-2\,\mathbb{E}_{p\sim\mu}\left[\frac{(1-p)q}{p+q-pq}\right]+\lambda,$$
$$\frac{d}{dq}\Big[q(1-q)\frac{d^2}{dq^2}F_\mu(q)\Big]=-2\,\mathbb{E}_{p\sim\mu}\left[\frac{(1-p)p}{(p+q-pq)^2}\right]<0$$
(outside the degenerate case $\mu$ supported on $\{0,1\}$ where (3) is $0$). So $q(1-q)F_\mu''(q)$ is **strictly decreasing** in $q$: either positive on $[0,a)$, zero at $a$, negative on $(a,1]$ for some $a$; or positive throughout; or negative throughout. Since $q(1-q)>0$ on $(0,1)$, $F_\mu''(q)$ has the same sign pattern. So $F_\mu$ is either strictly convex on $[0,a)$ and strictly concave on $(a,1]$ (for some $a$), or convex throughout, or concave throughout.

*Step 3: this forces the minimizing $\mu$ to be atomic on $\{v,1\}$.* If $\mu$ minimizes $\mathbb{E}_{q\sim\mu}[F_\mu(q)]$, it must put zero mass on the interior of the concave region $(a,1)$ (else push mass to the boundary, preserving $\mathbb{E}[q]$, decreasing $\mathbb{E}[F_\mu(q)]$), and its restriction to the convex region $[0,a]$ must be a single atom (else push mass to the center point, same reasoning). (Convex-only / concave-only cases are similar, simpler.) So the optimal $\mu$ is atomic, supported on $\{1\}$ and at most one other point.

*Step 4: explicit two-point computation.* Let $\mu$ put mass $w$ at $v$, mass $1-w$ at $1$. Then $\mathbb{E}_\mu[H(p)]=wH(v)$, $\mathbb{E}_{\mu\times\mu}[H(p+q-pq)]=w^2H(2v-v^2)$ (since $\mu\times\mu$ puts mass $w^2$ on $(v,v)$ where $p+q-pq=2v-v^2$, and $H(1\cdot\text{anything})$-type contributions vanish since $H(1)=0$). So (3) $\ge0$ iff $w^2H(2v-v^2)\ge\lambda wH(v)$, i.e.
$$w\cdot\frac{H(2v-v^2)}{H(v)}\ge\lambda.$$
LHS increasing in $w$; with expectation constraint $1-w(1-v)\le u$, the binding case is $w=\frac{1-u}{1-v}$, giving the requirement
$$(1-u)\min_{v\in[0,u]}\left(\frac{H(2v-v^2)}{H(v)(1-v)}\right)\ge\lambda. \tag{*}$$
By **Lemma 8** (below), $\frac{H(2v-v^2)}{H(v)(1-v)}$ is decreasing for $v<\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$, increasing for $v>\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$, and at $v=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$: $\frac{H(2v-v^2)}{H(v)}=1$ so $\frac{H(2v-v^2)}{H(v)(1-v)}=\frac{2}{\sqrt5-1}$. Hence
$$\min_{v\in[0,u]}\left(\frac{H(2v-v^2)}{H(v)(1-v)}\right)=\begin{cases}\frac{H(2u-u^2)}{H(u)(1-u)} & u\le\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\\ \frac{2}{\sqrt5-1} & u\ge\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\end{cases}=\frac{\lambda}{1-u},$$
so (*) holds with equality, proving the claim. $\blacksquare$

**Lemma 7.** For all $s\in(0,1)$: $H(s^2)<2sH(s)$.

*Proof.* $2(1-s)>(1-s^2)$ since the difference is $(1-s)^2>0$. Also, comparing power series of $-\log(1-x)$:
$$-s\log(1-s)=s^2+s^3/2+s^4/3+\dots \;>\; s^2+s^4/2+s^6/3+\dots = -\log(1-s^2)\cdot\text{[series in }s^2\text{ terms]}$$
(precisely: term-by-term, each term $s^{2k}/(2k{-}1)\cdot s$-ish dominates the corresponding term of $-\log(1-s^2)/2$ — the paper states it as $-s\log(1-s) > -\log(1-s^2)$, comparing the series $s^2+s^3/2+s^4/3+\cdots$ against $s^2+s^4/2+s^6/3+\cdots$). Then
$$H(s^2)=-s^2\log(s^2)-(1-s^2)\log(1-s^2) < -s^2\log(s^2)-2(1-s)\log(1-s^2)$$
$$< -s^2\log(s^2)-2s(1-s)\log(1-s)=2sH(s). \blacksquare$$

**Lemma 8.** The function $\dfrac{H(2v-v^2)}{H(v)(1-v)}$, $v\in(0,1)$, is decreasing for $v<\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ and increasing for $v>\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$.

*Proof.* Substitute $s=1-v$: the function becomes $F(s):=\dfrac{H(s^2)}{sH(s)}$. Need: $F$ decreasing for $s<\phi^{-1}=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$, increasing for $s>\phi^{-1}$ (here $\phi$ = golden ratio $\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}$; $\phi^{-1}=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$). Since $\phi^{-1}$ is already known to be a local min of $F$ (by direct calculation, or cited to Boppana [2]), it suffices to show $F'$ is **non-vanishing** on $(0,1)\setminus\{\phi^{-1}\}$.

Suppose $F'(s_0)=0$ at some $s_0$, $\beta:=F(s_0)$. Then $\frac{H(s^2)}{sH(s)}-\beta$ vanishes to *second* order at $s_0$ (single root of derivative ⟹ double root of $F-\beta$), so $H(s^2)-\beta sH(s)$ vanishes to second order at $s_0$. This function also vanishes to second order at $0$ and first order at $1$. If $s_0$ were the *only other* zero, $H(s^2)-\beta sH(s)$ would never change sign on $(0,1)\setminus\{s_0\}$, making $s_0$ a unique global min or max of $F$. But this is impossible: $\phi^{-1}$ is already known to be a global min (Boppana), and the global max is **not attained** since $F(s)<2$ for all $s\in(0,1)$ (Lemma 7) yet $F(s)\to2$ as $s\to0$ or $s\to1$ (direct calculation). So $H(s^2)-\beta sH(s)$ must have **at least one more zero**, giving total zero-order (with multiplicity) $\ge6$ on $[0,1]$; by Rolle's theorem its **third derivative** has $\ge3$ zeros in $[0,1]$.

Compute the third derivative directly:
$$\frac{d^3}{ds^3}H(s^2)=\frac{d^2}{ds^2}2sH'(s^2)=\frac{d}{ds}\big(2H'(s^2)+4s^2H''(s^2)\big)=12sH''(s^2)+8s^3H'''(s^2)$$
$$=\frac{-12s}{s^2(1-s^2)}+\frac{8s^3(1-2s^2)}{(s^2(1-s^2))^2}=\frac{-4-4s^2}{s(1-s^2)^2},$$
$$\frac{d^3}{ds^3}sH(s)=3H''(s)+sH'''(s)=\frac{-3}{s(1-s)}+s\cdot\frac{1-2s}{s^2(1-s)^2}=\frac{s(1-2s)-3s(1-s)}{s^2(1-s)^2}=\frac{s-2}{s(1-s)^2},$$
so
$$\frac{d^3}{ds^3}\big[H(s^2)-\beta sH(s)\big]=\frac{-4-4s^2-\beta(s-2)(1+s)^2}{s(1-s^2)^2}.$$
The numerator is a **cubic** polynomial in $s$ with leading coefficient $-\beta$ (negative) and value $-4+2\beta$ at $s=0$, which is **negative** since $\beta<2$ (Lemma 7). A cubic with negative leading coefficient and negative value at $0$ has **at least one negative real root**, hence **at most two roots in $[0,1]$** — contradicting the required $\ge3$ zeros. Contradiction. $\blacksquare$

### Section 2 — Proof Sketch: pushing past $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$

Goal (not fully carried out — a sketch): find $\delta>0$ such that every nonempty union-closed $\mathcal{F}$ has an element with proportion $\ge\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}+\delta$.

**Setup.** In addition to independent uniform $A,B\sim\mathcal{F}$, introduce $C$ uniform on $\mathcal{F}$ but **not necessarily independent from $A$**. Since $\mathcal{F}$ union-closed, $A\cup B, A\cup C\in\mathcal{F}$, so
$$H(A\cup B),\,H(A\cup C)\ \le\ \log|\mathcal{F}|=H(A).$$
Goal: show that if every $i$ is in $A$ with probability $<\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}+\delta$, then for some mixing weight $\alpha\in(0,1)$,
$$(1-\alpha)H(A\cup B)+\alpha H(A\cup C)\ >\ H(A),$$
which contradicts the two entropy inequalities above and forces some element to exceed the bound.

**The coupling.** At step $i$ (with $A_{<i},C_{<i}$ already fixed), define, in the paper's own exact wording: "Let $p_i$ be the proportion of $\{S\in\mathcal{F}\mid S_{<i}=C_{<i}\}$ that contain $i$ and let $r_i$ be the proportion of $\{S\in\mathcal{F}\mid S_{<i}=A_{<i}\}$ that contain $i$." (Note the labeling: $p_i$ is defined via $C$'s history and $r_i$ via $A$'s history — this is exactly as printed in the paper, not a transcription error.) We choose $A_i\sim$Bernoulli$(p_i)$ and $C_i\sim$Bernoulli$(r_i)$ — **correlated** so as to maximize the conditional entropy of $\max(A_i,C_i)$:

- If $p_i\ge1/2$ or $r_i\ge1/2$: draw $x\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]$; set $A_i=[x<p_i]$, $C_i=[x<r_i]$, giving
$$\Pr[\max(A_i,C_i)=1\mid A_{<i},C_{<i}]=\max(p_i,r_i).$$
- If $p_i,r_i<1/2$: draw $x\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]$; set $A_i=[x<p_i]$, $C_i=[0\le\tfrac12-x<r_i]$, giving
$$\Pr[\max(A_i,C_i)=1\mid A_{<i},C_{<i}]=\min(p_i+r_i,\tfrac12).$$

Since the conditional probability $i\in A$ is $p_i$, matching the uniform-on-$\mathcal{F}$ conditional structure, both $A$ and $C$ marginally remain uniform on $\mathcal{F}$. With $q_i=\Pr[i\in B\mid B_{<i}]$:
$$(1-\alpha)H((A\cup B)_{<i+1})+\alpha H((A\cup C)_{<i+1})$$
$$=(1-\alpha)H((A\cup B)_{<i})+\alpha H((A\cup C)_{<i}) + (1-\alpha)H((A\cup B)_{<i+1}\mid(A\cup B)_{<i})+\alpha H((A\cup C)_{<i+1}\mid(A\cup C)_{<i})$$
$$\ge(1-\alpha)H((A\cup B)_{<i})+\alpha H((A\cup C)_{<i})+(1-\alpha)H((A\cup B)_{<i+1}\mid A_{<i},B_{<i})+\alpha H((A\cup C)_{<i+1}\mid A_{<i},C_{<i})$$
$$\ge(1-\alpha)H((A\cup B)_{<i})+\alpha H((A\cup C)_{<i})+(1-\alpha)\mathbb{E}[H(p_i+q_i-p_iq_i)]+\alpha\,\mathbb{E}[H(\max(p_i,r_i,\min(p_i+r_i,1/2)))].$$
(NB: in this display the roles of $p_i,r_i$ inside the max-entropy term follow the paper's own — slightly loose — notation switch; the max is over the three quantities $p_i$, $r_i$, and $\min(p_i+r_i,1/2)$, reflecting the two coupling cases above.)

**Reduces the induction to proving:** for $p,q,r$ identically distributed $[0,1]$-valued, $p,q$ independent, $p,r$ not necessarily independent, all with expectation $\le\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}+\delta$:
$$(1-\alpha)\,\mathbb{E}[H(p+q-pq)] + \alpha\,\mathbb{E}[H(\max(p,r,\min(p+r,1/2)))] \;>\; \mathbb{E}[H(p)]. \tag{5}$$
Achieved "by choosing $\alpha$ sufficiently small and $\delta$ sufficiently small depending on $\alpha$."

**Heuristic argument for (5), as given (incomplete/non-quantitative):** From the proof of Lemma 3, the *only* measures $\mu$ on $[0,1]$ with $\mathbb{E}_{\mu\times\mu}[H(p+q-pq)]\le\mathbb{E}_\mu[H(p)]$ and expectation $\le\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ are the delta measure at $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$, or measures supported on $\{0,1\}$. So any weakly convergent sequence with entropy-ratio $\to\le1$ and expectation $\to\le\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ converges to one of these; it can't converge to a $\{0,1\}$-supported measure (nearby low-expectation measures have visibly high entropy ratio), so it converges to $\delta_{\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}}$. Hence for $\alpha,\delta$ small (depending on a target closeness $\epsilon$), any measure with ratio $\le1/(1-\alpha)$ and expectation $\le(\text{that ratio})+\delta$ must satisfy $\mathbb{E}[|p-\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}|]<\epsilon$. If both $p,r$ are so concentrated near $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$, then regardless of their correlation, $\max(p,r,\min(p+r,1/2))$ is usually close to $1/2$, so $\mathbb{E}[H(\max(\cdots))]/\mathbb{E}[H(p)]$ is close to $H(1/2)/H(\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2})>1$. Choosing $\delta$ small enough (given $\alpha$) that $\mathbb{E}[H(p+q-pq)]/\mathbb{E}[H(p)]$ is close enough to $1$ then makes (5) hold.

**Explicitly flagged as incomplete:** "It would be interesting to modify this argument to obtain an explicit value of $\delta$." No numeric $\delta$ is given anywhere in the paper.

## Sharpness of Theorem 2 / Lemma 3 (Examples 4 and 5)

**Example 4** (sharp for $u\le\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$; due to Gilmer). Events $\{i\in A\}_{i\in[n]}$ independent, each probability $u$. Then $A$ has entropy $nH(u)$; events $\{i\in A\cup B\}$ independent probability $2u-u^2$, so $H(A\cup B)=nH(2u-u^2)$. Ratio matches Theorem 2's first case exactly.

**Example 5** (sharp for $u\ge\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$). With probability $(1-u)\frac{2}{\sqrt5-1}$, choose each $i\in[n]$ into $A$ independently with probability $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$; with the complementary probability $1-(1-u)\frac2{\sqrt5-1}$, set $A=[n]$. Then $A$ has entropy $(1-u)\frac2{\sqrt5-1}\,nH(\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2})+O(1)$ (a convex combination of two distributions has entropy differing from the convex combination of their entropies by $\le\log2$). With probability $\left((1-u)\frac2{\sqrt5-1}\right)^2$, each $i\in A\cup B$ independently with probability $\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$; otherwise $A\cup B=[n]$. So $H(A\cup B)=\left((1-u)\frac2{\sqrt5-1}\right)^2nH(\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2})+O(1)$. Dividing, using $H\big(\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\big)=H\big(\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\big)$ (complementary-probability symmetry of $H$, since $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}+\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}=1$), the ratio equals $(1-u)\frac2{\sqrt5-1}$ exactly, matching Theorem 2's second case.

*Consequence stated explicitly:* this gives a **negative** answer to Gilmer's "first bulleted question on p.9" (whether $H(A\cup B)\ge\frac{H(2p-p^2)}{H(p)}H(A)$ always, for $\Pr[A_i{=}1]\le p$) when $u>\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$, while Theorem 2 gives a **positive** answer for $u\le\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$.

**Sharpness of Lemma 3 explicitly, in one line:** for $u\le\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$, the extremal random variable is the constant $u$; for $u\ge\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$, the extremal random variable equals $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ w.p. $(1-u)\frac2{\sqrt5-1}$ and $1$ w.p. $1-(1-u)\frac2{\sqrt5-1}$ — exactly the two-point atomic form derived abstractly in Step 3–4 of the Lemma 3 proof above.

## Disproof of Gilmer's Conjecture 1 — Proposition 6, full proof

Fix $\bar u<u$ with $d>\frac{H(2\bar u-\bar u^2)}{H(\bar u)}$.

**Construction.** Sample $k\ge0$ from Geometric($\theta$): $\Pr[k]=(1-\theta)\theta^k$. Given $k$, choose each $i\in[n]$ into $A$ independently with probability $1-(1-\bar u)^{k+1}$. ($\theta$ chosen small enough later.)

Marginal probability any element is in $A$: $\sum_{k\ge0}(1-\theta)\theta^k\big(1-(1-\bar u)^{k+1}\big) \to \bar u$ as $\theta\to0$; so for $\theta$ small this is $\le u$.

**Entropy lower bound for $A$:** $H(A)\ge\sum_{k\ge0}(1-\theta)\theta^k\,n\,H\big((1-\bar u)^{k+1}\big)$ (average of conditional entropies, a lower bound since conditioning on $k$ can only decrease entropy... more precisely this is $H(A\mid k)\le H(A)$, so this sum, which equals $H(A\mid k)$, lower-bounds $H(A)$).

**For independent $A,B$ from this law**, let $k_A,k_B$ be their respective $k$'s, $k'=k_A+k_B+1$. Then $k'$ is a positive integer with $\Pr[k']=(1-\theta)^2k'\theta^{k'-1}$, and each $i\in A\cup B$ independently with probability $1-(1-\bar u)^{k'+1}$.

**Entropy upper bound for $A\cup B$:** $H(A\cup B)\le H(k')+\sum_{k'\ge1}(1-\theta)k'\theta^{k'-1}\,n\,H\big((1-\bar u)^{k'+1}\big)$ ($H(k')$ finite, $O(1)$ in $n$).

**Ratio:**
$$\frac{H(A\cup B)}{H(A)}\ \le\ \frac{O(1)+\sum_{k'\ge0}(1-\theta)k'\theta^{k'-1}n\,H((1-\bar u)^{k'+1})}{\sum_{k\ge0}(1-\theta)\theta^k n\,H((1-\bar u)^{k+1})} = o(1)+\frac{\sum_{k'\ge0}(1-\theta)k'\theta^{k'-1}H((1-\bar u)^{k'+1})}{\sum_{k\ge0}(1-\theta)\theta^kH((1-\bar u)^{k+1})}.$$
As $\theta\to0$, numerator $\to H(2\bar u-\bar u^2)$, denominator $\to H(\bar u)$ (by a standard limiting/renewal-type argument on the geometric weighting), so for $\theta$ small the fraction is $<d$, and then for $n$ large the entropy ratio is $\le d$.

**KL-divergence bound (the crux — showing $D(A\cup B\|A)$ stays $O(1)$ even as $H(A)$ grows linearly):** By convexity of KL divergence,
$$D(A\cup B\|A)\ \le\ \sum_{k'\ge1}(1-\theta)k'\theta^{k'-1}\,D(S_{k'}\|A),$$
where $S_{k'}$ has each $i$ independently with probability $1-(1-\bar u)^{k'+1}$ (i.e. the law of $A\cup B$ conditioned on $k'$). $S_{k'}$'s law equals $A$'s law conditioned on the event $\{k=k'\}$, which has probability $(1-\theta)\theta^{k'}$; so $\Pr[S_{k'}=S]\le\frac1{(1-\theta)\theta^{k'}}\Pr[A=S]$ for all $S$, giving
$$D(S_{k'}\|A)\le\log\left(\frac1{(1-\theta)\theta^{k'}}\right)=-k'\log\theta-\log(1-\theta).$$
So
$$D(A\cup B\|A)\ \le\ \sum_{k'\ge1}(1-\theta)k'\theta^{k'-1}\big(-k'\log\theta-\log(1-\theta)\big)=O(1)$$
(a quadratic-in-$k'$ function summed against a geometrically decaying weight converges). $\blacksquare$

**Significance, stated by Gilmer's own update note (cross-referenced) and implicit here:** this shows incorporating the KL term $D(A\cup B\|A)$, as Gilmer's Conjecture 1 proposed, **cannot** improve the bound beyond what Lemma 3 already gives — there exist distributions with entropy ratio approaching the Lemma-3-optimal value while KL divergence stays bounded ($O(1)$) even though $H(A)$ grows linearly in $n$, precisely killing the mechanism Gilmer hoped to exploit (recall Gilmer's identity $D(A\cup B\|A)+H(A\cup B)=H(A)$ for **uniform** $A$ on a union-closed family — but this construction shows that for non-uniform $A$, having small $D(A\cup B\|A)$ does not force $H(A\cup B)$ close to $H(A)$ in the way needed).

## (c) Extremal / tightness summary

Both pieces of Theorem 2 / Lemma 3's piecewise bound are **exactly sharp** (Examples 4, 5 above), with the transition at $u=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ where the extremal distribution is the deterministic point mass at $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ itself — consistent with Gilmer's Examples 1–2 and the AHS two-point-mass reduction.

## (d) Remarks on limits of the method / open questions

- The paper is explicit that its own Section 2 argument is a **sketch**, not a complete proof, and gives **no explicit numeric improvement** over $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ — this was left open (later work by Cambie arXiv:2212.12500 and Yu arXiv:2212.00658 pursued explicit improvements using these ideas).
- **Question 9** is offered as the cleanest sufficient condition that would resolve the full conjecture: dropping the independence requirement between the two identically-distributed copies $A,B$ (while keeping $H(A)>0$ and all marginals $<1/2$) — a positive answer implies Frankl's conjecture outright. This directly identifies *independence* of the two samples as the specific structural assumption the entropy method currently relies on and which, if relaxed correctly, could break the $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ barrier.
- Gilmer's Conjecture 1 (KL-divergence strengthening) is **disproved** (Proposition 6), independently confirmed by Ellis's construction the following day — closing off that specific route.
- Lemma 8's proof required an error-correction: the acknowledgements explicitly note "Bhavik Mehta for pointing out an error in the original proof of Lemma 8" and credit Boppana's technique for the fix — i.e. the rigorous non-computer-assisted resolution of the golden-ratio monotonicity fact was nontrivial and errorprone even for an expert, foreshadowing Boppana's later fully clean symbolic proof (arXiv:2301.09664) of the closely related one-variable inequality.
