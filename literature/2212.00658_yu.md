## 0. Setup and notation (Section 1)

Each set $B\subseteq[n]:=\{1,2,\dots,n\}$ uniquely corresponds to an $n$-length sequence $x^{n}:=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\in\Omega^{n}$ with $\Omega:=\{0,1\}$, via $x_{i}=1$ if $i\in B$ and $x_{i}=0$ otherwise. A family $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of $[n]$ uniquely corresponds to a subset $A\subseteq\Omega^{n}$. Denote the (element-wise) OR operation for two finite $\Omega$-valued sequences as $x^{n}\vee y^{n}:=(x_{i}\vee y_{i})_{i\in[n]}$ with $x^{n},y^{n}\in\Omega^{n}$.

The family $\mathcal{F}$ is closed under the union operation (i.e., $F\cup G\in\mathcal{F},\forall F,G\in\mathcal{F}$) if and only if the corresponding set $A\subseteq\Omega^{n}$ is closed under the OR operation (i.e., $x^{n}\vee y^{n}\in A,\forall x^{n},y^{n}\in A$).

Let $A\subseteq\Omega^{n}$ be closed under the OR operation. Let $X^{n}:=(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n})$ be a random vector uniformly distributed on $A$, and denote $P_{X^{n}}=\mathrm{Unif}(A)$ as its distribution. Define

$$p_{A}:=\max_{i\in[n]}P_{X_{i}}(1) \tag{S1.Ex1}$$

where $P_{X_{i}}(1)$ is the proportion of the sets containing the element $i$ among all sets in $\mathcal{F}$.

### Conjecture 1 (Frankl Union-Closed Sets Conjecture)

> $p_{A}\geq 1/2$ *for any OR-closed set $A$.*

Equivalently: for any union-closed family $\mathcal{F}$, there exists an element contained in at least a proportion $1/2$ of the sets of $\mathcal{F}$.

Background cited in the paper: Gilmer (arXiv:2211.09055, 2022) proved the constant $0.01$; Gilmer conjectured his technique could be pushed to $\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}\approx 0.38197$, subsequently confirmed by Sawin (arXiv:2211.11504), Chase and Lovett (arXiv:2211.11689), Alweiss, Huang and Sellke (arXiv:2211.11731), and Pebody (arXiv:2211.13139). Chase and Lovett show $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ is best possible for an *approximate* version of the problem. Sawin further showed (without giving an explicit improved numeric value) that allowing the two random vectors to be *dependent* (rather than independent, as in Gilmer's construction) strictly improves the constant beyond $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$; this dependent-coupling technique had previously appeared in the present author's own works (Yu and Tan 2020; Yu 2021; Yu 2022).

## (a) Exact theorem/proposition/lemma statements

### Definitions needed to state the main results (Section 2)

**Couplings.** For a pair of distributions $(P_{X},P_{Y})$, a *coupling* of $(P_{X},P_{Y})$ is a joint distribution $P_{XY}$ whose marginals are respectively $P_{X},P_{Y}$. For a distribution $P_{X}$ defined on a finite alphabet $\mathcal{X}$, a coupling $P_{XX^{\prime}}$ of $(P_{X},P_{X})$ is called *symmetric* if $P_{XX^{\prime}}(x,y)=P_{XX^{\prime}}(y,x)$ for all $x,y\in\mathcal{X}$. Denote $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X})$ as the set of symmetric couplings of $(P_{X},P_{X})$. Denote $\delta_{x}$ as the Dirac measure with atom at $x$.

**Correlation coefficients.** For a joint distribution $P_{XY}$, the Pearson correlation coefficient between $(X,Y)\sim P_{XY}$ is

$$\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(X;Y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(X)\mathrm{Var}(Y)}},&\mathrm{Var}(X)\mathrm{Var}(Y)>0\\ 0,&\mathrm{Var}(X)\mathrm{Var}(Y)=0.\end{array}\right. \tag{S2.Ex2}$$

The *maximal correlation* between $(X,Y)\sim P_{XY}$ is

$$\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X;Y):=\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(f(X);g(Y)):=\sup_{f,g}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(f(X),g(Y))}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(f(X))\mathrm{Var}(g(Y))}},&\mathrm{Var}(f(X))\mathrm{Var}(g(Y))>0\\ 0,&\mathrm{Var}(f(X))\mathrm{Var}(g(Y))=0,\end{array}\right. \tag{S2.Ex3, S2.EGx1}$$

where the supremum is over all pairs of real-valued functions $(f,g)$ with $\mathrm{Var}(f(X))\mathrm{Var}(g(Y))<\infty$. Note $\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X;Y)\in[0,1]$, and $\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X;Y)=0$ iff $X,Y$ are independent. $\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X;Y)$ equals the second largest singular value of the matrix $\left[\frac{P_{XY}(x,y)}{\sqrt{P_{X}(x)P_{Y}(y)}}\right]_{(x,y)}$ (Witsenhausen 1975); the largest singular value is $1$, with eigenvectors $(\sqrt{P_X(x)})_x$ and $(\sqrt{P_Y(y)})_y$.

**The function $\varphi$.** For $p,q,\rho\in[0,1]$, denote

$$z_{1}:=pq-\rho\sqrt{p(1-p)q(1-q)}, \qquad z_{2}:=pq+\rho\sqrt{p(1-p)q(1-q)} \tag{S2.Ex6, S2.Ex7}$$

and

$$\varphi(\rho,p,q):=\mathrm{median}\left\{\max\{p,q,p+q-z_{2}\},1/2,\min\{p+q,p+q-z_{1}\}\right\}, \tag{1}$$

where $\mathrm{median}\,A$ denotes the median value of elements of the *multiset* $A$ (so e.g. $\mathrm{median}\{a,a,b\}=a$). Denote $h(a)=-a\log_{2}a-(1-a)\log_{2}(1-a)$ for $a\in[0,1]$ as the binary entropy function.

**The functional $\Gamma(t)$.** For $t>0$,

$$\Gamma(t):=\sup_{P_{\rho}}\inf_{P_{p}:\mathbb{E}h(p)>0,\mathbb{E}p\leq t}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\inf_{P_{pq}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{p}):\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(p;q)\leq\rho}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{p,q}h(\varphi(\rho,p,q))}{\mathbb{E}h(p)}\right], \tag{2}$$

where the supremum over $P_{\rho}$ and the infimum over $P_{p}$ are both taken over all finitely supported probability distributions on $[0,1]$.

### Theorem 1

> *If $\Gamma(t)>1$ for some $t\in(0,1/2)$, then $p_{A}\geq t$ for any OR-closed $A\subseteq\Omega^{n}$ (i.e., for any union-closed family $\mathcal{F}$, there exists an element contained in at least a proportion $t$ of the sets of $\mathcal{F}$).*

Equivalently, Theorem 1 states $p_{A}\geq t_{\max}$ for any OR-closed $A\subseteq\Omega^{n}$, where
$$t_{\max}:=\sup\{t\in(0,1/2):\Gamma(t)>1\}.$$

Remark (paper's own words): To compute $\Gamma(t)$ or its lower bounds numerically, one needs an upper bound on the cardinality of the support of $P_{p}$ in the outer infimum in (2), "since otherwise, infinitely many parameters are needed to optimize. This is left to be done in a future work." Proposition 1 below supplies a computable bound instead.

**Special cases.** Choosing $P_{\rho}=\delta_{0}$ makes the couplings constructed in the proof independent, and Theorem 1 reduces to Gilmer's bound. Choosing $P_{\rho}=\delta_{1}$ makes the couplings arbitrary. As suggested by Sawin, choosing $P_{\rho}=(1-\alpha)\delta_{0}+\alpha\delta_{1}$ yields an optimization over *mixtures* of independent and arbitrary couplings, giving Proposition 1.

Substituting $\rho=0$ and $\rho=1$ respectively into $\varphi(\rho,p,q)$:
$$\varphi(0,p,q)=p+q-pq, \tag{3}$$
$$\varphi(1,p,q)=\mathrm{median}\left\{\max\{p,q\},1/2,p+q\right\}, \tag{4}$$
using the facts (for all $p,q\in[0,1]$):
$$p+q-pq-\sqrt{p(1-p)q(1-q)}\leq\max\{p,q\},$$
and if $p+q\leq 1$: $\ p+q-pq+\sqrt{p(1-p)q(1-q)}\geq p+q$; otherwise: $\ 1/2<\max\{p,q\}\leq p+q-pq+\sqrt{p(1-p)q(1-q)}$.

Define
$$g(P_{pq},\alpha):=(1-\alpha)\mathbb{E}_{(p,q)\sim P_{p}^{\otimes 2}}h(p+q-pq)+\alpha\mathbb{E}_{(p,q)\sim P_{pq}}h(\varphi(1,p,q)) \tag{S2.Ex11-12}$$
and substitute $P_{\rho}=(1-\alpha)\delta_{0}+\alpha\delta_{1}$ into Theorem 1 to obtain Proposition 1.

### Proposition 1

> *For $t\in(0,1/2)$,*
> $$\Gamma(t)\geq\hat{\Gamma}(t):=\sup_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\inf_{\textrm{symmetric }P_{pq}:\mathbb{E}h(p)>0}\frac{g(P_{pq},\alpha)}{\mathbb{E}h(p)}, \tag{5}$$
> *where the infimum is taken over all distributions $P_{pq}$ of the form $(1-\beta)Q_{a_{1},a_{2}}+\beta Q_{b_{1},b_{2}}$ with*
> $$0\leq a:=\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}\leq t<b:=\frac{b_{1}+b_{2}}{2}\leq 1 \tag{6}$$
> *and $\beta=0$ or $\beta=\frac{t-a}{b-a}>0$ such that $\mathbb{E}h(p)>0$.* (Footnote: $\mathbb{E}h(p)=0$ iff $P_{pq}$ is a convex combination of $\delta_{(0,0)}$, $\delta_{(0,1)}$, $\delta_{(1,0)}$, and $\delta_{(1,1)}$.) *Here,*
> $$Q_{x,y}:=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{(x,y)}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{(y,x)} \tag{7}$$
> *with $\delta_{(x,y)}$ denoting the Dirac measure at $(x,y)$.*

This is the key finite-dimensional reduction: each admissible coupling $P_{pq}$ in the infimum has support on **at most 4 points** — $(a_1,a_2),(a_2,a_1),(b_1,b_2),(b_2,b_1)$ — i.e. is a mixture of at most two "doubled" Dirac atoms $Q_{a_1,a_2}$ and $Q_{b_1,b_2}$.

### Corollary 1

> *If $\hat{\Gamma}(t)>1$ for some $t\in(0,1/2)$, then $p_{A}\geq t$ for any OR-closed $A\subseteq\Omega^{n}$.*

### The numerical constant 0.38234

Direct quote (Section 2, paragraph after Corollary 1):

> "The lower bound in (5) without the cardinality bound on the support of $P_{pq}$ was given by Sawin (2022), which was used to show $p_{A}>\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}$. However, thanks to the cardinality bound, we can numerically compute the best bound on $p_{A}$ that can be derived using $\hat{\Gamma}(t)$. That is, $p_{A}\geq\hat{t}_{\max}$ for any OR-closed $A\subseteq\Omega^{n}$, where $\hat{t}_{\max}:=\sup\{t\in(0,1/2):\hat{\Gamma}(t)>1\}$. Numerical results [code on the author's homepage, https://leiyudotscholar.wordpress.com/] show that if we set $\alpha=0.035,t=0.38234$, then the optimal $P_{pq}=(1-\beta)Q_{a,a}+\beta Q_{a,1}$ with $a\approx 0.3300622$ and $\beta\approx 0.1560676$ which leads to the lower bound $\hat{\Gamma}(t)\geq 1.00000889$. Hence, $p_{A}\geq 0.38234$ for any OR-closed $A\subseteq\Omega^{n}$. This is slightly better than the previous bound $\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}\approx 0.38197$. The choice of $(\alpha,t)$ in our evaluation is nearly optimal. More decimal places of Sawin's bound (or equivalently, $\hat{t}_{\max}$) were computed by Cambie (2022), i.e., $0.382345533366702\leq\hat{t}_{\max}\leq 0.382345533366703$ which is attained by the choice $\alpha\approx 0.03560698136437784$."

So the precise, paper-stated result is:

$$p_A \;\geq\; \hat t_{\max} \;\geq\; 0.38234, \qquad c \ge 0.38234\ldots,$$

i.e. **there exists an element contained in at least a proportion $t\ge 0.38234$ of the sets of $\mathcal F$**, attained (up to the paper's numerical precision) at $\alpha=0.035$ with the near-extremal coupling $P_{pq}=(1-\beta)Q_{a,a}+\beta Q_{a,1}$, $a\approx0.3300622$, $\beta\approx0.1560676$, giving $\hat\Gamma(t)\geq 1.00000889$. The sharper (Cambie-computed) value of the same quantity $\hat t_{\max}$ satisfies $0.382345533366702\le \hat t_{\max}\le 0.382345533366703$, attained at $\alpha\approx 0.03560698136437784$.

## (b) Complete transcription of the proof of the main constant bound

### Lemma 1 (Product Construction of Couplings)

Citations inside the paper: (Yu and Tan, 2020, Lemma 9); (Yu, 2018, Corollary 3); (Beigi and Gohari, 2015, Lemma 6).

First, some preliminary conditional definitions (Section 3, before Lemma 1):

For two conditional distributions $P_{X|U},P_{Y|V}$, denote $\mathcal{C}(P_{X|U},P_{Y|V})$ as the set of conditional distributions $Q_{XY|UV}$ such that its marginals satisfy $Q_{X|UV}=P_{X|U},Q_{Y|UV}=P_{Y|V}$. The conditional (Pearson) correlation coefficient of $X$ and $Y$ given $U$ is defined by
$$\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(X;Y|U)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{cov}(X,Y|U)]}{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{var}(X|U)]}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{var}(Y|U)]}},&\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{var}(X|U)]\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{var}(Y|U)]>0,\\ 0,&\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{var}(X|U)]\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{var}(Y|U)]=0.\end{array}\right.$$
The conditional maximal correlation coefficient of $X$ and $Y$ given $U$ is defined by
$$\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X;Y|U)=\sup_{f,g}\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(f(X,U);g(Y,U)|U),$$
where the supremum is over all real-valued $f(x,u),g(y,u)$ with finite conditional variances. It has been shown in Yu (2018) that
$$\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X;Y|U)=\sup_{u:P_{U}(u)>0}\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X;Y|U=u),$$
where $\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X;Y|U=u)=\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X^{\prime};Y^{\prime})$ with $(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})\sim P_{XY|U=u}$.

**Lemma 1 statement.** *For any conditional distributions $P_{X_{i}|X^{i-1}},\,P_{Y_{i}|Y^{i-1}},\,i\in[n]$ and any*
$$Q_{X_{i}Y_{i}|X^{i-1}Y^{i-1}}\in\mathcal{C}(P_{X_{i}|X^{i-1}},P_{Y_{i}|Y^{i-1}}),\ \forall i\in[n], \tag{9}$$
*it holds that*
$$\prod_{i=1}^{n}Q_{X_{i}Y_{i}|X^{i-1}Y^{i-1}}\in\mathcal{C}\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}P_{X_{i}|X^{i-1}},\prod_{i=1}^{n}P_{Y_{i}|Y^{i-1}}\Big).$$
*Moreover, for $(X^{n},Y^{n})\sim\prod_{i=1}^{n}Q_{X_{i}Y_{i}|X^{i-1}Y^{i-1}}$, it holds that*
$$\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X^{n};Y^{n})=\max_{i\in[n]}\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X_{i};Y_{i}|X^{i-1},Y^{i-1}). \tag{10}$$

This is the "tensorization" property of maximal correlation used throughout: the maximal correlation of the whole product coupling equals the *max* (not sum) of the per-coordinate conditional maximal correlations. (Only the "$\le$" direction of (10) is actually used in the proof, as noted in Section 5.)

Denote $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X|U})$ as the set of symmetric conditional couplings of $(P_{X|U},P_{X|U})$ — a conditional coupling $P_{XX^{\prime}|UU^{\prime}}$ is *symmetric* if $P_{XX^{\prime}|UU^{\prime}}(x,y|u,v)=P_{XX^{\prime}|UU^{\prime}}(y,x|v,u)$ for all $x,y\in\mathcal{X},u,v\in\mathcal{U}$.

Applying Lemma 1 to symmetric couplings: if $Q_{X_{i}Y_{i}|X^{i-1}Y^{i-1}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X_{i}|X^{i-1}})$ satisfy $\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X_{i};Y_{i}|X^{i-1},Y^{i-1})\leq\rho$ for some $\rho>0$, then
$$\prod_{i=1}^{n}Q_{X_{i}Y_{i}|X^{i-1}Y^{i-1}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}P_{X_{i}|X^{i-1}}\Big),\qquad \rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X^{n};Y^{n})\leq\rho,$$
for any $\rho\in[0,1]$.

### The coupling framework and the reduction to $\Gamma_n(t)$

Denote $H(X)=-\sum_{x}P_{X}(x)\log P_{X}(x)$ as Shannon entropy. Let $A\subseteq\Omega^{n}$ be OR-closed with $|A|\geq 2$ (the case $|A|=1$ is trivial since $p_A=1$). Let $P_{X^{n}}=\mathrm{Unif}(A)$. So $H(X^{n})>0$, and by the chain rule,
$$H(X^{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}H(X_{i}|X^{i-1}).$$

**The coupling.** If $P_{X^{n}Y^{n}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X^{n}})$ — i.e. $(X^n,Y^n)$ is any *symmetric coupling* of $(P_{X^n},P_{X^n})$, so both $X^n$ and $Y^n$ are marginally $\mathrm{Unif}(A)$ but may be dependent, with the joint law invariant under swapping the two coordinates — then $Z^{n}:=X^{n}\vee Y^{n}\in A$ almost surely (because $A$ is closed under $\vee$ and both $X^n,Y^n\in A$ a.s.), where $(X^{n},Y^{n})\sim P_{X^{n}Y^{n}}$. So
$$H(Z^{n})\leq\log|A|=H(X^{n}),$$
and hence
$$\sup_{P_{X^{n}Y^{n}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X^{n}})}\frac{H(Z^{n})}{H(X^{n})}\leq 1.$$

**Relaxation to $\Gamma_n(t)$.** If $p_{A}\leq t$, then $P_{X_{i}}(1)\leq t,\forall i\in[n]$. Relaxing $P_{X^{n}}=\mathrm{Unif}(A)$ to *arbitrary* distributions such that $P_{X_{i}}(1)\leq t$, we obtain $\Gamma_{n}(t)\leq 1$ where
$$\Gamma_{n}(t):=\inf_{P_{X^{n}}:P_{X_{i}}(1)\leq t,\forall i}\sup_{P_{X^{n}Y^{n}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X^{n}})}\frac{H(Z^{n})}{H(X^{n})}. \tag{8}$$
In other words, if $\Gamma_{n}(t)>1$, then by contradiction $p_{A}>t$. **The remainder of the proof shows $\Gamma_{n}(t)\geq\Gamma(t)$, which implies Theorem 1.**

### The chain-rule / entropy induction that "single-letterizes" $\Gamma_n(t)$

Using Lemma 1 and the entropy chain rule, one obtains the following chain of inequalities (Eq. 11). Each step peels off one coordinate greedily:

$$\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{X^{n}Y^{n}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X^{n}}):\\ \rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X^{n};Y^{n})\leq\rho\end{subarray}}H(Z^{n})$$
$$\geq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{X^{n-1}Y^{n-1}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X^{n-1}}):\\ \rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X^{n-1};Y^{n-1})\leq\rho\end{subarray}}H(Z^{n-1})$$
$$\qquad+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{X_{n}Y_{n}|X^{n-1}Y^{n-1}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X_{n}|X^{n-1}}):\\ \rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X_{n};Y_{n}|X^{n-1},Y^{n-1})\leq\rho\end{subarray}}H(Z_{n}|Z^{n-1})$$
$$\geq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{X^{n-1}Y^{n-1}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X^{n-1}}):\\ \rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X^{n-1};Y^{n-1})\leq\rho\end{subarray}}H(Z^{n-1})$$
$$\qquad+\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{X^{n-1}Y^{n-1}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X^{n-1}}):\\ \rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X^{n-1};Y^{n-1})\leq\rho\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{X_{n}Y_{n}|X^{n-1}Y^{n-1}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X_{n}|X^{n-1}}):\\ \rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X_{n};Y_{n}|X^{n-1},Y^{n-1})\leq\rho\end{subarray}}H(Z_{n}|Z^{n-1})$$
$$\geq\cdots\cdots$$
$$\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{X^{i-1}Y^{i-1}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X^{i-1}}):\\ \rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X^{i-1};Y^{i-1})\leq\rho\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{X_{i}Y_{i}|X^{i-1}Y^{i-1}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X_{i}|X^{i-1}}):\\ \rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X_{i};Y_{i}|X^{i-1},Y^{i-1})\leq\rho\end{subarray}}H(Z_{i}|Z^{i-1}), \tag{11}$$

where the first inequality follows by Lemma 1 and the chain rule for entropies. In fact, in this derivation, the $i$-th distribution $P_{X_{i}Y_{i}|X^{i-1}Y^{i-1}}$ is chosen as a **greedy coupling** — it only maximizes the $i$-th objective function $H(Z_{i}|Z^{i-1})$, regardless of other $H(Z_{j}|Z^{j-1})$ with $j>i$ (although it indeed affects their values).

By the fact that conditioning reduces entropy,
$$H(Z_{i}|Z^{i-1})\geq H(Z_{i}|X^{i-1},Y^{i-1}).$$
Denote
$$g_{i}(P_{X^{i-1}},\rho):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{X^{i-1}Y^{i-1}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X^{i-1}}):\\ \rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X^{i-1};Y^{i-1})\leq\rho\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{X_{i}Y_{i}|X^{i-1}Y^{i-1}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X_{i}|X^{i-1}}):\\ \rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X_{i};Y_{i}|X^{i-1},Y^{i-1})\leq\rho\end{subarray}}H(Z_{i}|X^{i-1},Y^{i-1}). \tag{12}$$

The RHS of (11) is further lower bounded by $\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}(P_{X^{i-1}},\rho)$. Combining this with (8) and (11), and noting $\rho\in[0,1]$ is arbitrary, one obtains:

$$\Gamma_{n}(t)\geq\inf_{P_{X^{n}}:P_{X_{i}}(1)\leq t,\forall i}\frac{\sup_{\rho\in[0,1]}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}(P_{X^{i-1}},\rho)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}H(X_{i}|X^{i-1})}$$
$$=\inf_{P_{X^{n}}:P_{X_{i}}(1)\leq t,\forall i}\frac{\sup_{P_{\rho}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{\rho}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}(P_{X^{i-1}},\rho)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}H(X_{i}|X^{i-1})}$$
$$\geq\sup_{P_{\rho}}\inf_{P_{X^{n}}:P_{X_{i}}(1)\leq t,\forall i}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{P_{\rho}}g_{i}(P_{X^{i-1}},\rho)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}H(X_{i}|X^{i-1})}$$
$$\geq\sup_{P_{\rho}}\inf_{P_{X^{n}}:P_{X_{i}}(1)\leq t,\forall i}\min_{i\in[n]:H(X_{i}|X^{i-1})>0}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P_{\rho}}g_{i}(P_{X^{i-1}},\rho)}{H(X_{i}|X^{i-1})} \tag{13}$$
$$\geq\sup_{P_{\rho}}\inf_{P_{X^{j}}:H(X_{j}|X^{j-1})>0,P_{X_{j}}(1)\leq t}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P_{\rho}}g_{j}(P_{X^{j-1}},\rho)}{H(X_{j}|X^{j-1})},$$

where:
- (13) follows since $\frac{a+b}{c+d}\geq\min\{\frac{a}{c},\frac{b}{d}\}$ for $a,b\geq 0,c,d>0$, and $H(X_{i}|X^{i-1})=0$ implies $X_{i}$ is a function of $X^{i-1}$, hence $g_{i}(P_{X^{i-1}},\rho)=0$;
- the index $j$ in the last line is the optimal $i$ attaining the minimum in (13).

**This is precisely how "the past" (earlier coordinates $X^{i-1},Y^{i-1}$) is handled**: it is absorbed via the greedy per-coordinate coupling and the min-ratio bound, reducing an $n$-coordinate multi-letter problem to a single "worst" coordinate $j$.

### Collapsing to a two-variable (single-letter) problem

Denote $X=X_{j},Y=Y_{j},U=X^{j-1},V=Y^{j-1}$, and $Z=X\lor Y$. Then
$$\Gamma_{n}(t)\geq\sup_{P_{\rho}}\inf_{P_{UX}:H(X|U)>0,P_{X}(1)\leq t}\mathbb{E}_{P_{\rho}}\left[\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{UV}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{U}):\\ \rho_{\mathrm{m}}(U;V)\leq\rho\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{XY|UV}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X|U}):\\ \rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X;Y|U,V)\leq\rho\end{subarray}}\frac{H(Z|U,V)}{H(X|U)}\right]. \tag{14}$$

**Simplifying the inner supremum.** Denote
$$p=P_{X|U}(1|U),\quad q=P_{Y|V}(1|V),\quad r=P_{XY|UV}(1,1|U,V). \tag{15}$$
So
$$P_{XY|UV}(\cdot|U,V)=\begin{bmatrix}1+r-p-q&q-r\\ p-r&r\end{bmatrix} \qquad\text{with}\qquad \max\{0,p+q-1\}\leq r\leq\min\{p,q\}.$$

Note (data-processing / matrix-singular-value characterisation of maximal correlation for binary variables):
$$\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X;Y|U,V)=\sup_{u,v:P_{UV}(u,v)>0}\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X_{u};Y_{v})=\sup_{u,v:P_{UV}(u,v)>0}\left|\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(X_{u};Y_{v})\right|=\sup_{u,v:P_{UV}(u,v)>0}\frac{\left|r-pq\right|}{\sqrt{p(1-p)q(1-q)}}, \tag{16}$$
where $(X_{u},Y_{v})\sim P_{XY|U=u,V=v}$, using the fact that the maximal correlation coefficient between two binary random variables equals the absolute value of their Pearson correlation coefficient (Anantharam et al. 2013). So $\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X;Y|U,V)\leq\rho$ is equivalent (a.s.) to $\frac{\left|r-pq\right|}{\sqrt{p(1-p)q(1-q)}}\leq\rho$, and also equivalent to $z_{1}\leq r\leq z_{2}$ a.s.

The inner supremum in (14) can then be rewritten as
$$\sup_{P_{XY|UV}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{s}}(P_{X|U}):\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(X;Y|U,V)\leq\rho}H(Z|U,V)=\mathbb{E}_{p,q}\sup_{\max\{0,p+q-1,z_{1}\}\leq r\leq\min\{p,q,z_{2}\}}h(p+q-r).$$

Since $h$ is increasing on $[0,1/2]$ and decreasing on $[1/2,1]$, the optimal $r$ attaining the supremum, denoted $r^{*}$, is the **median** of $\max\{0,p+q-1,z_{1}\}$, $p+q-1/2$, and $\min\{p,q,z_{2}\}$, which implies
$$p+q-r^{*}=\varphi(\rho,p,q)$$
— recovering exactly the definition of $\varphi$ in (1). So the inner supremum in (14) equals $\dfrac{\mathbb{E}_{p,q}h(\varphi(\rho,p,q))}{\mathbb{E}h(p)}$ (once divided by the denominator $H(X|U)$, see below).

**Final identifications, closing the proof of Theorem 1.** Three observations:

1. $H(X|U)=\mathbb{E}h(p)$, and $P_{X}(1)=\mathbb{E}p$.
2. By the data processing inequality, $\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(p;q)\leq\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(U;V)$ holds since $p,q$ are respectively functions of $U,V$ (see (15)).
3. $P_{UV}$ is symmetric, and $p,q$ are obtained from $U,V$ via the *same* function $P_{X|U}(1|\cdot)$ (since $P_{X|U}=P_{Y|V}$ by symmetry of $P_{XY|UV}$). Hence $P_{pq}$ is symmetric as well.

Substituting all of these into (14) yields
$$\Gamma_{n}(t)\geq\Gamma(t),$$
which — combined with $\Gamma_n(t)\le 1$ whenever $p_A\le t$, shown earlier — completes the proof of Theorem 1. $\blacksquare$

### Proof of Proposition 1 — the Krein–Milman / extreme-point reduction to support size $\le 4$

By choosing $P_{\rho}=(1-\alpha)\delta_{0}+\alpha\delta_{1}$ in (2), we obtain
$$\Gamma(t)\geq\sup_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\inf_{\textrm{symmetric }P_{pq}:\mathbb{E}h(p)>0,\mathbb{E}p\leq t}\frac{g(P_{pq},\alpha)}{\mathbb{E}h(p)}.$$

**Concavity.** $P_{pq}\mapsto g(P_{pq},\alpha)$ is concave, since by (Alweiss, Huang and Sellke 2022, Lemma 5), $P_{p}\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{(p,q)\sim P_{p}^{\otimes 2}}h(p+q-pq)$ is concave, and $P_{pq}\mapsto P_{p}$ is linear (a linear pushforward of a concave functional composed with a linear map is concave).

**Krein–Milman step.** Let $B$ be a finite subset of $[0,1]$. Let $\mathcal{P}_{B}$ be the set of symmetric distributions $P_{pq}$ concentrated on $B^{2}$ such that $\mathbb{E}p\leq t$. **By the Krein–Milman theorem, $\mathcal{P}_{B}$ is equal to the closed convex hull of its extreme points.** These extreme points are of the form $(1-\beta)Q_{a_{1},a_{2}}+\beta Q_{b_{1},b_{2}}$ with $0\leq a\leq t<b\leq 1$ and $\beta=0$ or $\beta=\frac{t-a}{b-a}$, where $a:=\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2},b:=\frac{b_{1}+b_{2}}{2}$, and $Q_{x,y}:=\frac12\delta_{(x,y)}+\frac12\delta_{(y,x)}$ as in (6)–(7). By Carathéodory's theorem, the convex hull of these extreme points is closed (in the weak topology, equivalently the relative topology on the probability simplex). So every $P_{pq}$ supported on a finite set $B^{2}\subseteq[0,1]^{2}$ such that $\mathbb{E}p\leq t$ is a convex combination of the extreme points above, i.e.
$$P_{pq}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}Q_{i}$$
where $Q_{i},i\in[k]$ are extreme points, and $\gamma_{i}>0$, $\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}=1$.

**Reduction via concavity + min inequality.** For this distribution,
$$\frac{g(P_{pq},\alpha)}{\mathbb{E}h(p)}=\frac{g(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}Q_{i},\alpha)}{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}\mathbb{E}_{Q_{i}}h(p)}\geq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}g(Q_{i},\alpha)}{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}\mathbb{E}_{Q_{i}}h(p)}\geq\min_{i:\mathbb{E}_{Q_{i}}h(p)>0}\frac{g(Q_{i},\alpha)}{\mathbb{E}_{Q_{i}}h(p)}$$
where the first inequality uses **concavity of $g(\cdot,\alpha)$** applied to the convex combination $\sum\gamma_i Q_i$ (this is the linearity/convexity argument requested: $g$ of the average is $\ge$ the average of $g$ by concavity), and the second uses the same $\frac{\sum a_i}{\sum c_i}\ge\min_i a_i/c_i$ type inequality as before. In the last line, one uses the fact that $\mathbb{E}_{Q_{i}}h(p)=0$ implies $Q_{i}=\delta_{(0,0)}$ (note $t<1/2$), and hence $g(Q_{i},\alpha)=0$ for such $i$ — so those terms drop out of the minimum without affecting validity.

**Conclusion.**
$$\Gamma(t)\geq\sup_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\inf_{P_{pq}:\mathbb{E}h(p)>0}\frac{g(P_{pq},\alpha)}{\mathbb{E}h(p)}, \tag{17}$$
where the infimum is taken over distributions $P_{pq}$ of the form $(1-\beta)Q_{a_{1},a_{2}}+\beta Q_{b_{1},b_{2}}$ with $0\leq a\leq t<b\leq 1$ and $\beta=0$ or $\beta=\frac{t-a}{b-a}>0$ such that $\mathbb{E}h(p)>0$ (recall the definitions of $a,b$ in (6)). This is exactly Proposition 1 / Eq. (5). $\blacksquare$

**Why this yields a computable (finite-dimensional) optimization.** Each extreme-point coupling $Q_i = (1-\beta)Q_{a_1,a_2}+\beta Q_{b_1,b_2}$ has support on at most the four points $(a_1,a_2),(a_2,a_1),(b_1,b_2),(b_2,b_1)\in[0,1]^2$ — i.e. **support size $\le 4$ per coordinate** in the pair $(p,q)$ — parametrized by only the finitely many real numbers $(a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2,\beta)$ (equivalently $(a,b,\beta)$ once symmetrized, subject to (6) and $\beta\in\{0,\tfrac{t-a}{b-a}\}$). This is precisely what makes $\hat\Gamma(t)$ in (5) numerically optimizable (a finite-dimensional $\sup_\alpha\inf_{a,b,\beta}$ problem), in contrast to $\Gamma(t)$ in (2), whose outer infimum ranges over *all* finitely-but-arbitrarily supported distributions $P_p$ on $[0,1]$ and is therefore not directly computable without such a cardinality bound.

### The explicit finite-dimensional optimization problem solved numerically

Putting it together, the number $0.38234$ (and Cambie's sharper $\hat t_{\max}\in[0.382345533366702,\,0.382345533366703]$) is obtained by numerically solving:

$$\hat{\Gamma}(t)=\sup_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\ \inf_{\substack{0\le a\le t< b\le 1\\ \beta\in\{0,\ (t-a)/(b-a)\}}}\ \frac{g\big((1-\beta)Q_{a,a}+\beta Q_{a,1},\ \alpha\big)}{\mathbb{E}h(p)}$$

(using the near-extremal family $Q_{a,a}$, $Q_{a,1}$ that the numerics select), where
$$g(P_{pq},\alpha)=(1-\alpha)\,\mathbb{E}_{(p,q)\sim P_p^{\otimes2}}\, h(p+q-pq)\ +\ \alpha\,\mathbb{E}_{(p,q)\sim P_{pq}}\, h\big(\mathrm{median}\{\max\{p,q\},\,1/2,\,p+q\}\big),$$
$$Q_{x,y} = \tfrac12\delta_{(x,y)}+\tfrac12\delta_{(y,x)}, \qquad h(a) = -a\log_2 a - (1-a)\log_2(1-a),$$
subject to $t\in(0,1/2)$ ranging over candidate proportion lower bounds, and $\hat t_{\max} := \sup\{t\in(0,1/2):\hat\Gamma(t)>1\}$.

Then $p_A \ge \hat t_{\max}$ for all OR-closed $A$. The paper reports $\hat t_{\max}\ge 0.38234$ (attained near $\alpha=0.035$, $a\approx0.3300622$, $\beta\approx0.1560676$), matching/slightly improving on Sawin's $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\approx0.38197$, with Cambie's independently-computed refinement pinning $\hat t_{\max}$ to $15$ significant digits: $0.382345533366702\le\hat t_{\max}\le0.382345533366703$ at $\alpha\approx0.03560698136437784$.

## (c) Extremal / near-extremal distributions given in the paper

1. **Gilmer's construction recovered as the $\alpha=0$ (i.e. $P_\rho=\delta_0$) special case**: independent couplings, $\varphi(0,p,q)=p+q-pq$ (Eq. 3).
2. **Sawin's fully-dependent construction as the $\alpha=1$ (i.e. $P_\rho=\delta_1$) special case**: arbitrary couplings, $\varphi(1,p,q)=\mathrm{median}\{\max\{p,q\},1/2,p+q\}$ (Eq. 4).
3. **The near-optimal mixture found numerically by the present paper**: $\alpha=0.035$, $t=0.38234$, optimal $P_{pq}=(1-\beta)Q_{a,a}+\beta Q_{a,1}$ with
   $$a\approx 0.3300622,\qquad \beta\approx 0.1560676,$$
   giving $\hat{\Gamma}(t)\geq 1.00000889$, hence $p_A\ge 0.38234$.
4. **Cambie's refined optimum** (cited, not independently re-derived in this paper): the supremizing $\alpha$ is
   $$\alpha\approx 0.03560698136437784,$$
   giving $0.382345533366702\leq\hat{t}_{\max}\leq0.382345533366703$.
5. **General extreme-point family** (Section 4): every optimal/extremal symmetric coupling $P_{pq}$ (with $\mathbb{E}p\le t$, supported on a finite $B\subset[0,1]$) is a convex combination of couplings of the form $(1-\beta)Q_{a_1,a_2}+\beta Q_{b_1,b_2}$ with $0\le a\le t<b\le1$ and $\beta\in\{0,\frac{t-a}{b-a}\}$, i.e. mixtures of two symmetrized Dirac pairs, one with average value $\le t$ and one with average value $>t$.

## (d) Authors' remarks on limitations, open questions, and possible next steps (Section 5, "Discussion")

Direct paraphrase/quotation of the paper's own discussion:

1. **Information-theoretic reformulation as a coding problem.** The union-closed sets conjecture has a natural information/coding-theoretic reading: consider the memoryless OR multi-access channel $(x^{n},y^{n})\in\Omega^{2n}\mapsto x^{n}\lor y^{n}\in\Omega^{n}$. One wants a nonempty code $A\subseteq\Omega^{n}$ generating two *independent* inputs $X^n,Y^n\sim\mathrm{Unif}(A)$ satisfying the input constraint $\mathbb{E}[X_i]\le t,\ \forall i\in[n]$, such that the output $X^n\lor Y^n\in A$ a.s. The conjecture states such a code exists iff $t\ge1/2$.

2. **Approximate vs. exact constraints.** "It is well-known that information-theoretic techniques usually work very well for problems with 'approximate' constraints, e.g., the channel coding problem with the asymptotically vanishing error probability constraint (or the approximate version of the union-closed sets problem introduced in Chase and Lovett (2022)). It is hard to say whether information-theoretic techniques are sufficient to prove sharp bounds for problems with 'exact' constraints, e.g., the zero-error coding problem (or the original version of the union-closed sets conjecture)." — i.e. the author flags an intrinsic possible limitation of the whole information-theoretic approach for resolving the *exact* $1/2$ conjecture.

3. **Open: single-letterization of $\Gamma_n(t)$.** As an intermediate result, $\Gamma_{n}(t)>1$ implies $p_{A}>t$ for any OR-closed $A\subseteq\Omega^{n}$, where $\Gamma_n(t)$ (Eq. 8) is expressed in *multi-letter* (dimension-dependent) form. "By the super-block coding argument, it is verified that given $t>0$, $\lim_{n\to\infty}\Gamma_{n}(t)$ exists. It is interesting to investigate this limit, and prove a single-letter (dimension-independent) expression for it." This connects to the earlier-flagged open problem in Section 2: computing $\Gamma(t)$ itself (not just $\hat\Gamma(t)$) numerically requires bounding the cardinality of the support of $P_p$ in the outer infimum of (2), which is explicitly "left to be done in a future work."

4. **Generalizing beyond maximal correlation.** "For simplicity, in this paper, we only consider the maximal correlation coefficient as the constraint function. In fact, the maximal correlation coefficient used here can be replaced by other functionals. The key property of the maximal correlation coefficient we used in this paper is the 'tensorization' property, i.e., (10) (in fact, only '$\leq$' part of (10) was used in our proof)." Other measures of correlation satisfying the same tensorization property include the hypercontractivity constant, the strong data processing inequality constant, or more generally $\Phi$-ribbons (citing Ahlswede and Gács 1976; Raginsky 2016; Beigi and Gohari 2018). "Following the same proof steps given in this paper, one can obtain various variants of Theorem 1 with the maximal correlation coefficient replaced by other quantities, as long as these quantities satisfy the tensorization property."

5. **Rényi entropy obstruction.** "Another potential direction is to replace the Shannon entropy with a class of more general quantities, Rényi entropies. However, unfortunately Rényi entropies do not satisfy the chain rule (unlike the Shannon entropy), which leads to a serious difficulty in single-letterizing the corresponding multi-letter bound like $\Gamma_{n}(t)$ in (8) (i.e., in making the multi-letter bound dimension-independent)." — an explicit, named technical obstruction to a natural generalization.
