## (a) Abstract and main theorem statements, exact

**Abstract.** "It is a well-known conjecture, sometimes attributed to Frankl, that for any family of sets which is closed under the union operation, there is some element which is contained in at least half of the sets. Gilmer [1] was the first to prove a constant bound, showing that there is some element contained in at least 1% of the sets. They state in their paper that the best possible bound achievable by the same method is $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\approx38.1\%$. This note achieves that bound by finding the optimum value, given a binary variable $X$ potentially depending on some other variable $S$ with a given expected value $E(X)$ and conditional entropy $H(X|S)$ of the conditional entropy of $H(X_1\cup X_2|S_1,S_2)$ for independent readings $X_1,S_1$ and $X_2,S_2$."

**Theorem 1.** Let $A$ and $B$ denote independent samples from a distribution over subsets of $[n]$. Assume that for all $i\in[n]$, $\Pr[i\in A]\le\alpha$ for some value $0<\alpha\le\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$. Then
$$H(A\cup B)\ \ge\ \frac{H(\alpha^2)}{H(\alpha)}\,H(A).$$

**Theorem 2.** Let $\mathcal{F}\subseteq2^{[n]}$ be a union-closed family, $\mathcal{F}\ne\emptyset$. Then there exists $i\in[n]$ that is contained in at least a $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\approx38.1\%$ fraction of the sets in $\mathcal{F}$.

*Proof of Theorem 2.* "Suppose not. Let $A$ and $B$ be drawn independently and uniformly from $\mathcal{F}$. Then, since $\Pr[i\in A]$ is rational for each $i$, there exists some $\alpha<\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ for which $\Pr[i\in A]\le\alpha$ for all $i\in[n]$. Then from Theorem 1 it follows that $H(A\cup B)\ge\frac{H(\alpha^2)}{H(\alpha)}H(A)>H(A)$. But this causes a contradiction, as $A$ is drawn from the uniform distribution on $\mathcal{F}$ and $A\cup B$ is drawn from some other distribution on $\mathcal{F}$ and the uniform distribution has the maximum entropy." $\blacksquare$

**Lemma 3 (Main Lemma).** For $1\le i\le n$, let $p_i,v_i\in[0,1]$ with $\sum_ip_i=1$ and $\sum_ip_iv_i\le\alpha$ for some $0<\alpha\le\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$. Then
$$\sum_{i,j}p_ip_j\,H(v_i+v_j-v_iv_j)\ \ge\ \frac{H(2\alpha-\alpha^2)}{H(\alpha)}\sum_ip_i\,H(v_i).$$
"The proof [of Theorem 1 from Lemma 3] follows exactly as the Proof of Theorem 1 from Lemma 1 in [Gilmer]."

**Lemma 4 (Lemma 3 rewritten via $w_i=1-v_i$, using $H(1-x)=H(x)$).** For $1\le i\le n$, $p_i,w_i\in[0,1]$ with $\sum_ip_i=1$ and $\sum_ip_iw_i\ge\beta$ for some $1>\beta\ge\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$. Then
$$\sum_{i,j}p_ip_j\,H(w_iw_j)\ \ge\ \frac{H(\beta^2)}{H(\beta)}\sum_ip_i\,H(w_i).$$

**Lemma 5.** $\dfrac{H(x^2)}{H(x)}$ is increasing on $[0,1]$. *[Proof marked "To be proven" in the paper — no proof is given.]*

**Lemma 6.** $\dfrac{H(x^2)}{xH(x)}$ is increasing on $\left[\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2},1\right]$. *[Proof also marked "To be proven" in the paper — no proof is given. Both facts are later given rigorous symbolic proofs by Boppana (arXiv:2301.09664) and are essentially the AHS/Sawin monotonicity facts about $H(x^2)/(xH(x))$.]*

**Lemma 7.** $f(x):=H(x)/x$ is continuous, onto, and decreasing from $(0,1]$ to $(0,\infty)$.

*Proof.* $f'(x)=\dfrac{\log(1-x)}{x^2}$, negative on the domain. $H(1)/1=0$; as $x\to0$, $H(x)/x>-\log x\to\infty$. $\blacksquare$

Since $f$ is a continuous decreasing bijection $(0,1]\to(0,\infty)$, it has an inverse $g:(0,\infty)\to(0,1]$, $f(g(y))=y$, differentiable with $g'(y)=1/f'(g(y))$.

**Lemma 8.** For all $0<\alpha<1$, the function $x\mapsto f(\alpha\,g(x))$ is convex.

*Proof.* $\dfrac{d}{dx}f(\alpha g(x)) = f'(\alpha g(x))\alpha g'(x) = \dfrac{\log(1-\alpha g(x))}{\alpha^2g(x)^2}\cdot\dfrac{\alpha}{f'(g(x))} = \dfrac{\log(1-\alpha g(x))}{\alpha^2g(x)^2}\cdot\dfrac{\alpha g(x)^2}{\log(1-g(x))} = \dfrac{\log(1-\alpha g(x))}{\alpha\log(1-g(x))}$.

Need this **increasing in $x$**. Since $g(x)$ is decreasing, equivalent to showing $\dfrac{\log(1-\alpha y)}{\alpha\log(1-y)}$ is **decreasing** in $y$ on $(0,1]$. Its $y$-derivative:
$$\frac{d}{dy}=\frac{-\alpha/(1-\alpha y)\cdot\alpha\log(1-y)+\alpha/(1-y)\cdot\log(1-\alpha y)}{\alpha^2\log(1-y)^2} = \frac{-\alpha(1-y)\log(1-y)+(1-\alpha y)\log(1-\alpha y)}{\alpha\log(1-y)^2(1-y)(1-\alpha y)^2} \cdot(1-\alpha y)$$
which after simplification (dividing numerator and denominator by suitable factors, as in the paper) becomes
$$\frac{-(1-y)\log(1-y)/y+(1-\alpha y)\log(1-\alpha y)/(\alpha y)}{\log(1-y)^2(1-y)(1-\alpha y)^2/y}.$$
So convexity of $f(\alpha g(x))$ for **all** $0<\alpha<1$ reduces to: $-(1-\alpha y)\log(1-\alpha y)/(\alpha y) > -(1-y)\log(1-y)/y$ for all $0<\alpha<1$, i.e. to **$-(1-z)\log(1-z)/z$ decreasing on $[0,1]$**.

Using the quotient-derivative test (if $g'(x)>0,g(x)>0$ then $f(x)/g(x)$ is decreasing whenever $f(x)/g(x)\ge f'(x)/g'(x)$): $-(1-z)\log(1-z)/z$ is decreasing if
$$-(1-z)\log(1-z)/z \ \ge\ 1+\log(1-z),$$
which rearranges to $\log(1-z)\le -z$, i.e. $1-z\le e^{-z}$ — **true for all $z$** (standard inequality). $\blacksquare$

### Section 5 — Optimising Joint Entropy

**Goal.** For any distribution $p$ on $[0,1]$, find a distribution $q$ on $[0,1]$ with **at most one non-zero point** in its support, matching $\mathbb{E}_p[X]$, $\mathbb{E}_p[H(X)]$, but with $\mathbb{E}_{q,q}[H(X_1X_2)]\le\mathbb{E}_{p,p}[H(X_1X_2)]$ — i.e. replacing any distribution by a one-atom (plus possibly an atom at 0, implicit) distribution can only *decrease* the pairwise joint quantity $\mathbb{E}[H(X_1X_2)]$ while preserving mean and mean-entropy. This is proved by iteratively merging pairs of atoms.

**Lemma 9 (the merge step).** Given $p_1,p_2,x_1,x_2\in(0,1]$, there exist $q,y$ with $p_1x_1+p_2x_2=qy$ and $p_1H(x_1)+p_2H(x_2)=qH(y)$, and for such $q,y$:
1. $q\le p_1+p_2$;
2. for all $0\le z\le1$: $p_1H(zx_1)+p_2H(zx_2)\ge qH(zy)$;
3. $p_1^2H(x_1^2)+2p_1p_2H(x_1x_2)+p_2^2H(x_2^2)\ge q^2H(y)^2$ *(sic — as printed in the source; almost certainly should read $q^2H(y^2)$, matching the use made of it in the proof of Lemma 4 below, where the corresponding step is written correctly as $q^2H(y^2)$)*.

*Proof.* Since $f=H(t)/t$ is onto, choose $y$ with
$$\frac{H(y)}{y}=f(y)=\frac{p_1x_1}{p_1x_1+p_2x_2}f(x_1)+\frac{p_2x_2}{p_1x_1+p_2x_2}f(x_2)=\frac{p_1H(x_1)+p_2H(x_2)}{p_1x_1+p_2x_2}.$$
Set $q=\dfrac{p_1x_1+p_2x_2}{y}$; the two defining equalities hold immediately. Since $f$ monotonic and $H(y)/y$ is a convex combination of $H(x_1)/x_1,H(x_2)/x_2$, $y$ lies between $x_1,x_2$, giving $q\le p_1+p_2$ (property 1).

For property 2: fix $z$. By Lemma 8, $x\mapsto f(zg(x))$ is convex. With $p=\frac{p_1x_1}{p_1x_1+p_2x_2}$, since $f(y)=pf(x_1)+(1-p)f(x_2)$, convexity gives $f(zg(f(y)))\le pf(zg(f(x_1)))+(1-p)f(zg(f(x_2)))$; since $g,f$ are inverse, this is $f(zy)\le pf(zx_1)+(1-p)f(zx_2)$, i.e.
$$\frac{H(zy)}{y}\le\frac{p_1x_1}{p_1x_1+p_2x_2}\frac{H(zx_1)}{x_1}+\frac{p_2x_2}{p_1x_1+p_2x_2}\frac{H(zx_2)}{x_2}$$
$$\Rightarrow H(zy)\le p_1x_1\frac{H(zx_1)}{x_1}+p_2x_2\frac{H(zx_2)}{x_2}=p_1H(zx_1)+p_2H(zx_2).$$
Property 3 follows by applying property 2 at $z=y,x_1,x_2$ in turn:
$$q^2H(y^2)\le q\big(p_1H(yx_1)+p_2H(yx_2)\big)=p_1(qH(yx_1))+p_2(qH(yx_2))$$
$$\le p_1\big(p_1H(x_1)^2+p_2H(x_1x_2)\big)+p_2\big(p_1H(x_1x_2)+p_2H(x_2)^2\big)$$
*(sic — again $H(x_1)^2,H(x_2)^2$ as literally printed; presumably intended as $H(x_1^2),H(x_2^2)$ throughout, matching the earlier and later statements)*. $\blacksquare$

**Theorem 10.** The optimization goal stated above holds for every distribution $p$ on $[0,1]$.

*Proof.* First for finite support: if $p$ has $\ge2$ nonzero support points $x_1,x_2$ with masses $p_1,p_2$, use Lemma 9 to replace them by mass $q$ at $y$ and mass $p_1+p_2-q$ at $0$ — still a valid distribution ($0\le q\le p_1+p_2$), smaller support, same mean and mean-entropy (by the defining equalities), and $\mathbb{E}_{p',p'}[H(X_1X_2)]\le\mathbb{E}_{p,p}[H(X_1X_2)]$ (property 3). Iterate until support size 1 (nonzero) is reached. Finite-support distributions are dense (in the relevant weak sense) and the expectations continuous, so the result extends to all distributions. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary 11.** For $0<t,u<1$ with $u\le H(t)$: the minimum possible value of $\mathbb{E}_{p,p}[H(X_1X_2)]$ over distributions $p$ with $\mathbb{E}_p[X]=t$, $\mathbb{E}_p[H(X)]=u$ is
$$\frac{t^2H(v^2)}{v^2}, \qquad v=g(u/t).$$

*Proof.* Achieved by $q$ = mass $t/v$ at $v$, mass $1-t/v$ at $0$ (valid since $f(v)=u/t\le H(t)/t=f(t)\Rightarrow t<v$ as $f$ decreasing). Optimality: given any $p$ with the stated mean/mean-entropy, Theorem 10 gives $q$ with $\le1$ nonzero point, same mean $t$, mean-entropy $u$, and no larger $\mathbb{E}_{q,q}[H(X_1X_2)]$; since $\mathbb{E}_q[X]=t\ne0$, $q$ has a nonzero point $v'$ with mass $p'$, so $t=p'v'$, $u=p'H(v')$, giving $f(v')=u/t=f(v)\Rightarrow v'=v$ (injectivity), $p'=t/v$ — exactly the constructed $q$. $\blacksquare$

**Proof of Lemma 4 (using Corollary 11 and Lemma 6).** Given $p_i,w_i$ as in Lemma 4's hypotheses, set $t=\sum_ip_iw_i$. Concavity of $H$ gives $0\le\sum_ip_iH(w_i)\le H(t)$. Set $u=\sum_ip_iH(w_i)$, $v=g(u/t)$. Corollary 11 gives
$$\sum_i\sum_jp_ip_jH(w_iw_j)\ \ge\ t^2H(v^2)/v^2.$$
Since $f(v)=u/t\le H(t)/t=f(t)\Rightarrow t<v$. By **Lemma 6**, $\dfrac{H(v^2)}{vH(v)}\ge\dfrac{H(t^2)}{tH(t)}$ (this is "the only place in the proof we use that $\beta\ge\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$" — i.e., this is where the hypothesis $\beta\ge\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$ of Lemma 4, forcing $t=\sum p_iw_i\ge\beta\ge\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$, actually gets used, via Lemma 6's monotonicity domain). So
$$\sum_i\sum_jp_ip_jH(w_iw_j)\ \ge\ t^2\frac{H(v^2)}{v^2} = t\cdot\frac{H(v^2)}{vH(v)}\cdot\frac{tH(v)}{v} = tu\frac{H(v^2)}{vH(v)} \ge tu\frac{H(t^2)}{tH(t)} = \frac{H(t^2)}{H(t)}\sum_ip_iH(w_i) \ge \frac{H(\beta^2)}{H(\beta)}\sum_ip_iH(w_i),$$
the last step by **Lemma 5**. $\blacksquare$

*(Note: Lemma 4 is what actually establishes Theorem 1/Lemma 3's bound with the sharp coefficient $\frac{H(\alpha^2)}{H(\alpha)}$ — under the change of variables $v_i=1-w_i$, Lemma 4's conclusion $\sum p_ip_jH(w_iw_j)\ge\frac{H(\beta^2)}{H(\beta)}\sum p_iH(w_i)$ is literally Lemma 3 rewritten, as stated in the text between Lemma 3 and Lemma 4.)*

## (c) Extremal / tightness discussion

Pebody's method **achieves exactly** $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$, explicitly framed in the abstract as reaching the ceiling Gilmer himself identified: "They [Gilmer] state in their paper that the best possible bound achievable by the same method is $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\approx38.1\%$. This note achieves that bound." So Pebody's contribution is **not** a numeric improvement beyond $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ — it matches Chase–Lovett, Sawin (first case), and AHS at exactly the same constant, via a different optimization route (the $f(x)=H(x)/x$, $g=f^{-1}$, and iterative atom-merging machinery of Lemma 9/Theorem 10/Corollary 11, rather than AHS's concavity-of-$F$-and-Krein–Milman route or Sawin's direct calculus-of-variations route). The tight case, as in the other papers, is the two-point (atom at $0$ plus atom at $v=\alpha$ or $\beta$) distribution, matching the extremal examples in Gilmer/AHS/Sawin.

Theorem 1's hypothesis restricts to $\alpha\le\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ only — Pebody's paper (unlike Sawin's Theorem 2) does **not** give a stated result for $\alpha>\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$; the note focuses solely on reaching the ceiling identified by Gilmer, not on the regime beyond it.

## (d) Remarks on limits of the method / open questions

- **Two lemmas are left as unproved assertions in the paper as posted**: Lemma 5 ($H(x^2)/H(x)$ increasing on $[0,1]$) and Lemma 6 ($H(x^2)/(xH(x))$ increasing on $[\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2},1]$) both carry the placeholder proof "To be proven." These are essentially equivalent to the monotonicity facts independently established (via computer-assisted or lengthy symbolic means) in AHS's Appendix A (Claim 3) and Sawin's Lemma 8, and later given a fully clean symbolic proof by Boppana (arXiv:2301.09664), whose paper explicitly quotes Pebody's "to be proven" as one of the historical gaps it closes.
- The paper contains apparent minor **typographical inconsistencies** in Lemma 9's statement (property 3 as printed reads $H(x_1)^2,H(x_2)^2,q^2H(y)^2$ where the surrounding logic and later usage clearly intend $H(x_1^2),H(x_2^2),q^2H(y^2)$) — flagged above at the relevant point; these do not appear to affect the validity of the argument, only its literal typesetting.
- No discussion is given in this paper of extending past $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ (contrast Sawin's Section 2 sketch) — Pebody's stated goal is solely to reach the ceiling with a cleaner/more systematic optimization argument (full characterization of the extremal joint-entropy-minimizing distribution via Theorem 10/Corollary 11, rather than an ad hoc two-point reduction).
