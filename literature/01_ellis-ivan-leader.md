# Ellis–Ivan–Leader, "Small sets in union-closed families"

- **arXiv:** 2201.11484 [math.CO]
- **Authors:** David Ellis, Maria-Romina Ivan, Imre Leader
- **Submitted:** 27 Jan 2022; latest version 23 Jan 2023 (published *Journal of the LMS*)

## What it shows

For an exact union-closed family F with a *unique* smallest set S (|S| = k), the
natural hope is that some element of S itself is frequent, since S is contained
in every set of the "up-set" generated by unioning with S. Ellis–Ivan–Leader
show this hope is false in general: for every ε > 0 there exist union-closed
families with a unique smallest set S such that **no element of S lies in more
than an ε fraction of the sets of F**. Quantitatively, they construct families
where each element of the size-k smallest set S appears in at most

  (1 + o(1)) · log₂(k) / (2k)

fraction of the members of F — i.e. exponentially small in k, not bounded below
by any absolute constant.

They also exhibit specific small union-closed families containing such a "small
set" S for which they were **unable to verify the conjecture directly** (later
resolved computationally by Pulaj and Wood — see the Kalai/offsprings survey,
arXiv:2306.12351).

## Relevance: limits of "small set" / averaging approaches

Much of the pre-2022 literature (Poonen, Reimer, Vučković–Živković, etc.)
attacks the conjecture via structural leverage on the smallest sets or on
average set size, on the intuition that a small "generating" set should force
frequency on its own elements. Ellis–Ivan–Leader is the clean formal
counterexample to that intuition: **the smallest set of a union-closed family
carries no guarantee that any of its own elements are frequent.** Any proof
strategy of the shape "find a small set, then show one of its elements must be
popular" is dead as a general-purpose route — the frequent element (if one
exists, as conjectured) must in general come from *outside* the smallest set.

This is a genuine structural fact about union-closed families (it does not
obviously relativize to "approximately union-closed" families, since the
construction relies on exact closure under union to force S to remain the
*unique* smallest set as unions accumulate) — see SYNTHESIS_lane4.md.
