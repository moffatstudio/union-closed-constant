## (a) Abstract and main theorem statements, exact

**Abstract.** "We verify an explicit inequality conjectured in [Gilmer], thus proving that for any nonempty union-closed family $\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}$, some $i\in[n]$ is contained in at least a $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\approx0.38$ fraction of the sets in $\mathcal{F}$. One case, an explicit one-variable inequality, is checked by computer calculation."

**Setup.** Let $\mathcal{M}_\phi$ be probability measures $\mu\in\mathcal{P}([0,1])$ with expectation $\phi$. Define
$$F(\mu)=\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mu\times\mu}H(xy)-\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu}H(x) \tag{1}$$
where $H(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x)$, $\log$ natural log. $F$ is continuous in the weak topology and $\mathcal{M}_\phi$ compact, so $F$ attains a minimizer.

**Theorem 1.** For all $\phi\in[0,1]$, the minimum of $F(\mu)$ over $\mathcal{M}_\phi$ is attained at some $\mu$ supported on at most two points. Furthermore, if a minimizer is supported on exactly two points, then one of the points is $0$.

**Definition.** $S=\{\phi\in[0,1] : \phi H(x^2)\ge xH(x)\ \forall x\in[\phi,1]\}$, $\phi^*=\min(S)$ (well-defined: the defining condition is monotone in $\phi$, and $S$ is closed).

**Theorem 2.** The union-closed conjecture holds with constant $1-\phi^*$: for any non-empty union-closed $\mathcal{F}\subseteq2^{[n]}$, some $i\in[n]$ is contained in at least a $1-\phi^*$ fraction of the sets in $\mathcal{F}$.

Throughout the paper $\varphi:=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$ (note: this is $1/$golden-ratio, i.e. the *positive root of $x^2+x-1=0$*, distinct from Boppana's/Sawin's $\phi=\frac{\sqrt5+1}{2}$ golden ratio — $\varphi = 1/\phi_{\text{golden}}$).

**Claim 3** (verified numerically in the Appendix, to error $10^{-3}$, rigorizable via interval arithmetic). If $x\in[\varphi,1]$, then $\varphi H(x^2)\ge xH(x)$, with equality iff $x\in\{\varphi,1\}$.

**Claim 4.** $\phi^*=\varphi$.

Combining: Theorem 2 + Claim 4 give the union-closed conjecture holds with constant $1-\varphi=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$. "This is a natural barrier for the method of [Gilmer] as explained therein." Claim 3 is noted to have appeared previously in a different context in Boppana's 1985 Boolean-formula paper.

**Related work note (verbatim substance):** concurrently and independently, Chase–Lovett, Sawin, and Pebody also proved the union-closed conjecture with constant $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$. Sawin also outlined an argument to push the bound by an additional small constant (later made explicit by Yu using AHS's Lemma 5, and by Cambie). Sawin and Ellis independently found counterexamples to Gilmer's Conjecture 1 (the KL-divergence strengthening), which would have implied the full conjecture with constant $\tfrac12$.

## (b) Complete proof transcription

### Section 2 — Reduction to Two Point Masses

**Lemma 5 (concavity of $F$).** $F$ is concave on $\mathcal{M}_\phi$: $pF(\mu_1)+(1-p)F(\mu_2)\le F(p\mu_1+(1-p)\mu_2)$ for all $\mu_1,\mu_2\in\mathcal{M}_\phi$, $p\in[0,1]$.

*Proof.* Let $\gamma(x)=\mu([0,x])$ (CDF). Then $\gamma(1)=1$ and $\phi=\int_0^1 x\,\mu(dx)=1-\int_0^1\gamma(x)\,dx$, so
$$\int_0^1\gamma(x)\,dx=1-\phi. \tag{3}$$
Integration by parts:
$$\int_0^1 H(x)\mu(dx)=H(x)\gamma(x)\Big|_0^1-\int_0^1 H'(x)\gamma(x)\,dx=\int_0^1\left(\log\frac{x}{1-x}\right)\gamma(x)\,dx.$$
Similarly, computing $\int_0^1 H(xy)\mu(dy)$ by parts:
$$\int_0^1 H(xy)\mu(dy)=H(x)+\int_0^1\left(x\log\frac{xy}{1-xy}\right)\gamma(y)\,dy,$$
and then $\int_0^1\left(x\log\frac{xy}{1-xy}\right)\mu(dx) = \log\frac{y}{1-y}-\int_0^1\left(\frac1{1-xy}+\log\frac{xy}{1-xy}\right)\gamma(x)\,dx$.
Combining (double integral):
$$\iint_{[0,1]^2}H(xy)\mu(dx)\mu(dy)=\int_0^1 H(x)\mu(dx)+\int_0^1\gamma(y)\int_0^1 x\log\frac{xy}{1-xy}\mu(dx)\,dy$$
$$=2\int_0^1\left(\log\frac{x}{1-x}\right)\gamma(x)\,dx-\iint_{[0,1]^2}\left(\frac1{1-xy}+\log\frac{xy}{1-xy}\right)\gamma(x)\gamma(y)\,dx\,dy.$$
So, writing $F(\gamma)=F(\mu)$,
$$F(\gamma)=\int_0^1\left(\log\frac{x}{1-x}\right)\gamma(x)\,dx-\iint_{[0,1]^2}\left(\log x+\log y+\frac1{1-xy}+\log\frac1{1-xy}\right)\gamma(x)\gamma(y)\,dx\,dy.$$
Show this is **concave in $\gamma$**. First integral is linear. The $\log x,\log y$ cross terms are linear because, by (3), $\iint(\log x)\gamma(x)\gamma(y)\,dx\,dy=(1-\phi)\int(\log x)\gamma(x)\,dx$. Removing these, need convexity of $\iint\left(\frac1{1-xy}+\log\frac1{1-xy}\right)\gamma(x)\gamma(y)\,dx\,dy$. Both $\frac1{1-xy}$ and $\log\frac1{1-xy}$ have power-series expansions $\sum_{k\ge0}a_kx^ky^k$ with $a_k\ge0$, so it suffices that $\iint x^ky^k\gamma(x)\gamma(y)\,dx\,dy=\left(\int_0^1 x^k\gamma(x)\,dx\right)^2$ is convex in $\gamma$ — true since it is the square of a linear functional. $\blacksquare$

**Lemma 6.** $\arg\min_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_\phi}F(\mu)$ contains some $\mu$ supported on at most two points.

*Proof.* Follows from Lemma 5 + Krein–Milman ($\mathcal{M}_\phi$ compact convex in weak-*, extreme measures supported on $\le2$ points — cited to Winkler 1988). Elementary self-contained version also given: take a minimizer $\mu$, approximate by finite-support $\hat\mu\in\mathcal{M}_\phi$ with $F(\hat\mu)\le F(\mu)+\varepsilon$, atoms $a_1<\dots<a_k$, cumulative weights $0=b_0<b_1<\dots<b_k=1$. If $k\ge3$, perturb three consecutive atoms $a_1,a_2,a_3$ by
$$\hat\mu_\eta(a_1)=b_1{-}b_0+\eta(a_3{-}a_2),\quad \hat\mu_\eta(a_2)=b_2{-}b_1-\eta(a_3{-}a_1),\quad \hat\mu_\eta(a_3)=b_3{-}b_2+\eta(a_2{-}a_1),$$
unchanged elsewhere. There is a range $\eta\in[-c_1,c_2]$ keeping $\hat\mu_\eta\in\mathcal{M}_\phi$; $\eta\mapsto F(\hat\mu_\eta)$ is concave (Lemma 5), so its minimum over the interval is at an endpoint, each of which has support size $\le k-1$; take the better endpoint. Iterate down to support size $\le2$, giving $\tilde\mu$ with $F(\tilde\mu)\le F(\hat\mu)\le F(\mu)+\varepsilon$; take a subsequential weak limit as $\varepsilon\to0$. $\blacksquare$

### Section 3 — Optimization over Two Point Masses

**Lemma 7.** If $\mu$ is supported on exactly two points, neither $0$, then $\mu$ is **not** a minimizer of $F$ over $\mathcal{M}_\phi$.

*Proof.* Suppose $\mu=p\delta_x+(1-p)\delta_y$ minimizes $F$, $0<y<x<1$ distinct, $0<p<1$. Any $z\in[0,1]$ writes as $z=qx+(1-q)y$, $q\in\mathbb{R}$ (possibly negative). For small $t\ge0$, $\mu+t\delta_z-tq\delta_x-t(1-q)\delta_y\in\mathcal{M}_\phi$, so by minimality
$$\lim_{t\to0^+}\frac{F(\mu+t\delta_z-tq\delta_x-t(1-q)\delta_y)-F(\mu)}{t}\ge0.$$
This limit equals $f(z)-qf(x)-(1-q)f(y)\ge0$ (4), for $f(w):=2[pH(xw)+(1-p)H(yw)]-H(w)$. So $f$ lies above the chord through $(x,f(x)),(y,f(y))$. Since $f$ is smooth and $x,y$ interior:
(a) $f'(x)=f'(y)=\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}$;
(b) $f''(x),f''(y)\ge0$;
and (a) forces (c) $f''(z)\le0$ for some $z\in[y,x]$ (Rolle on $f'$).
Compute, using $H'(w)=\log\frac{1-w}{w}$:
$$f'(z)=2\Big[px\log\frac{1-xz}{xz}+(1-p)y\log\frac{1-yz}{yz}\Big]-\log\frac{1-z}{z},$$
$$f''(z)=-2\Big[\frac{px}{z(1-xz)}+\frac{(1-p)y}{z(1-yz)}\Big]+\frac1{z(1-z)}.$$
Let $g(z):=z(1-z)(1-xz)(1-yz)f''(z)$ — same sign as $f''(z)$, and a **quadratic** in $z$ with leading coefficient $-2pxy-2(1-p)xy+xy=-xy<0$. Then $g(x),g(y)\ge0$ and $g(z)\le0$ for some $z\in(x,y)$ can hold only if $g\equiv0$ on $[x,y]$ — impossible since $g$ has nonzero leading coefficient. Contradiction.

Case $x=1$, $y>0$: similar; $g(z)$ is now a multiple of $1-z$, so $g(1)=0\ge0$; same quadratic argument with negative leading coeff $-y<0$ forces $g$ positive strictly between $y$ and $1$, contradicting that $f$ (continuous, smooth on $(0,1)$, staying above the chord) must have $f''(z)\le0$ somewhere in $(y,1)$. ($f''(1)$ need not exist and is not used.) $\blacksquare$

### Section 4 — Conclusion

**Proof of Theorem 1.** Follows from Lemmas 6 and 7. $\blacksquare$

**Lemma 8.** $\phi^*\ge\varphi$.

*Proof.* $H(\varphi^2)=H(\varphi)$ (special golden-ratio identity, since $\varphi^2=1-\varphi$). If $\phi<\varphi$ then $\phi H(\varphi^2)<\varphi H(\varphi)$, so $\phi\notin S$. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary 9.** If $\phi\ge\phi^*$, then $F(\mu)\ge0$ for all $\mu\in\mathcal{M}_\phi$.

*Proof.* By Theorem 1 suffices to check $\mu=p\delta_x+(1-p)\delta_0$, $p=\phi/x$, $x\in[\phi,1]$ (includes $\mu=\delta_\phi$ at $x=\phi$). By monotonicity of $S$'s defining condition, $\phi\in S$. So
$$F(\mu)=\frac{\phi^2}{x^2}H(x^2)-\frac{\phi}{x}H(x)=\frac{\phi}{x^2}\big(\phi H(x^2)-xH(x)\big)\ge0. \blacksquare$$

**Corollary 10 (the tight, sharpened Gilmer Lemma 1).** If $\{p_c\}_{c\in\mathcal{S}}\subset[0,1]$ finite and $c$ a random variable on $\mathcal{S}$ with $\mathbb{E}_c[p_c]\le1-\phi^*$, and $c'$ an iid copy, then
$$\mathbb{E}_{c,c'}[H(p_c+p_{c'}-p_cp_{c'})]\ge\mathbb{E}_c[H(p_c)].$$

*Proof.* Let $\mu$ = distribution of $x=1-p_c$; $\phi=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu}[x]\ge\phi^*$. By Corollary 9,
$$\mathbb{E}_{c,c'}[H(p_c+p_{c'}-p_cp_{c'})]-\mathbb{E}_c[H(p_c)]=\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mu\times\mu}H(xy)-\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu}H(x)=F(\mu)\ge0. \blacksquare$$

**Connection back to Frankl's conjecture (stated explicitly, generalizing Gilmer's argument):** "We recall Gilmer's ingenious insight was that given a union-closed family $\mathcal{F}\subseteq2^{[n]}$, if $A,A'$ are independent uniformly random samples from $\mathcal{F}$, then $A\cup A'\in\mathcal{F}$ is not uniformly random and thus has strictly smaller entropy. On the other hand, Corollary 10 can be applied element-by-element to show that $A\cup A'$ actually has equal or larger entropy." Theorem 2 follows from Corollary 10 "by the same argument as in [Gilmer, Proof of Theorem 1]" (i.e. the chain-rule/data-processing induction identical in structure to Gilmer's Theorem 1 proof, replacing his $1.26$-constant Lemma 1 with the tight Corollary 10).

**Proof of Claim 4 (assuming Claim 3).** Claim 3 gives $\varphi\in S$, so $\phi^*\le\varphi$; combined with Lemma 8's $\phi^*\ge\varphi$, equality. $\blacksquare$

### Appendix A — Proof of Claim 3 (numerical verification)

Define $G(x)=\varphi H(x^2)-xH(x)$; want $G(x)\ge0$ for $x\in[\varphi,1]$. Split into $I_1=[\varphi,0.77]$, $I_2=[0.76,0.98]$, $I_3=[0.98,1]$.

**On $I_1$ (Claim 11).** $G(\varphi)=G'(\varphi)=0$ (verified directly: $G'(\varphi)=2(1-\varphi-\varphi^2)\log\varphi=0$ since $\varphi^2+\varphi=1$). Suffices to show $G$ convex on $I_1$. Second derivative $G''(x)=L(x)/(1-x^2)$ where
$$L(x)=2\varphi(1-x^2)\log(x^{-2}-1)-4\varphi-2x^2\log x+2(x^2-1)\log(1-x)+x+2\log x+1.$$
Lipschitz-constant bound on each term over $I_1$ gives total Lipschitz constant $\le15.5$. A $1/400$-dense grid check with $L(x)\ge0.04$ (in fact $\ge0.09$ at all sampled multiples of $1/200$ from $0.6$ to $0.77$, per an included table) suffices to conclude $L\ge0$, hence $G$ convex, hence $G\ge0$ on $I_1$.

**On $I_2$ (Claim 13, using Lemma 12).** Write $G=g_1-g_2$, $g_1(x)=\varphi H(x^2)$ (clearly decreasing on $I_2$), $g_2(x)=xH(x)$.

*Lemma 12: $g_2$ decreasing on $[5/7,1]\supseteq I_2$.* $g_2'(x)=H(x)+x\log\frac{1-x}{x}=2x\log\frac1x-(2x-1)\log\frac1{1-x}$; suffices $g_2'(5/7)\le0$ (monotone comparison argument), which reduces to $\frac{3}{10}\log(7/2)\ge\log(7/5)$, i.e. $(7/5)^{10/3}\le7/2$ — verified via $(7/5)^3\le14/5$ and $7/5\le(5/4)^3=(\frac{7/2}{14/5})^3$.

Given monotonicity, a grid of 97 computer-evaluated points $x_1<\dots<x_{97}$ in $[5/7,0.98]$ with $g_1(x_{i+1})-g_2(x_i)\ge2/1000$ at each step (table given to $10^{-4}$ precision) implies $g_2(x)\le g_2(x_i)\le g_1(x_{i+1})\le g_1(x)$ for $x\in[x_i,x_{i+1}]$, covering $I_2$.

**On $I_3$ (Proposition 14).** Let $x=1-\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon\in[0,0.02]$. Using Taylor bounds $\varepsilon(\log\frac1\varepsilon+1-\varepsilon)\le H(\varepsilon)\le\varepsilon(\log\frac1\varepsilon+1)$:
$$g_1(x)=\varphi H(2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2)\ge\varphi\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)\Big(\log\tfrac1\varepsilon-\log(2-\varepsilon)+(1-\varepsilon)^2\Big),$$
$$g_2(x)=(1-\varepsilon)H(\varepsilon)\le\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\Big(\log\tfrac1\varepsilon+1\Big).$$
Dividing by $\varepsilon$, reduces to
$$\big((2\varphi-1)+(1-\varphi)\varepsilon\big)\log\tfrac1\varepsilon \;\ge\; (1-\varepsilon)\big(1-\varphi(1-\varepsilon)(2-\varepsilon)\big)+\varphi(2-\varepsilon)\log(2-\varepsilon).$$
RHS $\le2\varphi\log2=(\sqrt5-1)\log2$ (using $\varphi(1-\varepsilon)(2-\varepsilon)\ge1$); LHS $\ge(2\varphi-1)\log\frac1\varepsilon\ge(\sqrt5-2)\log50$ (for $\varepsilon\le0.02$). Then $(\sqrt5-2)\log50\ge(\sqrt5-1)\log2$ follows from $\log_2(50)\ge5.5\ge3+\sqrt5=(\sqrt5-1)/(\sqrt5-2)$. **Equality holds iff $\varepsilon=0$, i.e. $x=1$.**

**Proof of Claim 3.** Combine Claims 11, 13, Proposition 14. $\blacksquare$

## (c) Tightness / extremal case

Equality in Claim 3 (hence in the whole chain giving the $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ bound) occurs **exactly** at $x\in\{\varphi,1\}$, i.e. the extremal two-point measure is $\mu=\delta_\varphi$ (all mass at $x=\varphi=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$) — corresponding via $\phi=1-\psi$ notation to Gilmer's Examples 1/2 with $p=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$: iid-coordinate or all-or-nothing families with per-element probability exactly $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$. The reduction (Theorem 1) shows the worst case for $F$ is **always** two-point with one atom at $0$; combined with Claim 3's endpoint equality, the entropy-method inequality $F(\mu)\ge0$ becomes tight precisely at the golden-ratio-linked point.

## (d) Remarks on limits of the method / open questions

- Explicitly: "This is a natural barrier for the method of [Gilmer] as explained therein" — i.e. AHS confirm that $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ is not merely their achieved bound but the **provable optimum of the entropy-method inequality itself** (Theorem 1's reduction to $\le2$-point measures, one atom at $0$, is airtight — no slack remains to extract from Corollary 10/the one-variable inequality alone).
- Sawin's proof-sketch (see Sawin transcription) of going *beyond* $\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ requires abandoning the pure independent-$(A,B)$-sample setup and introducing a third, correlated sample $C$ — i.e., stepping outside the exact structure Theorem 1/Corollary 10 optimizes.
- Sawin and Ellis's counterexamples to Gilmer's Conjecture 1 (KL-divergence strengthening) are noted as closing off that particular route to the full conjecture.
- Claim 3's numerical/computer-assisted verification was later given a **fully symbolic proof** by Boppana (arXiv:2301.09664) — see that transcription; this removes the only non-symbolic step in the present paper.
